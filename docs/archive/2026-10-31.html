October 30: Three centralized exchanges add 13 traditional-asset perpetuals in two days

Binance, OKX and Bybit added 13 USDT perpetual contracts linked to stocks, ETFs and biotech assets on October 29 and 30. The contracts trade around the clock with leverage capped at 20× or 25×. None of the announcements disclosed volume, open interest, deviations while underlying markets were closed or regional availability.

October 28: Robinhood’s quarterly filing confirms that Stock Tokens confer no shareholder rights

Robinhood’s quarterly filing identifies Stock Tokens as tokenized debt securities issued by Robinhood Assets Jersey. They convey economic exposure to underlying shares without legal or beneficial rights in the underlying issuers. They therefore fall outside the SEC’s innovation exemption for tokenized NMS stock carrying the same rights as the listed stock.

October 28: CME clarifies that round-the-clock crypto futures trading still defers weekend clearing

CME’s updated rules allow round-the-clock trading in most crypto futures and options with a two-minute daily pause on weekdays and a two-hour Saturday maintenance window. Weekend trades are assigned to the next business day. Clearing, settlement and regulatory reporting also wait until that day. Continuous trading has not removed the business-day dependency in the back office.

Collateral, wallets and cross-chain distribution

October 29: Kamino adds four Backpack tokenized equities as collateral

Kamino added Backpack’s tokenized MU, SKHY, SPCX and SNDK shares to isolated lending markets where users can borrow USDC. Each asset has its own loan-to-value ratio, liquidation threshold and borrowing cap. While the U.S. cash market is closed, its oracle carries forward the last price. Round-the-clock onchain transfers do not remove that risk.

October 28: Phantom connects eligible U.S. users to Kalshi prediction markets

Phantom has enabled qualified users in the United States to trade Kalshi event contracts using Phantom Cash. Kalshi is still responsible for account opening, KYC, execution, result determination, and payments. This service is not available in some states. The wallet reduces friction in entering and moving funds, but it does not take over market access and settlement responsibilities.

October 30: Circle enables non-USDC tokens to integrate with CCTP on the Arc testnet

Circle lets issuers connect their ERC-20 tokens to CCTP on Arc testnet. Burning and minting preserve token identity across chains. Issuers can configure owners, operators, pause roles, rate limits and per-transfer caps. Testnet availability establishes neither the scope nor the timing of support on Arc mainnet.

October 28: GitHub unifies Copilot cloud sessions and sandboxes across interfaces

GitHub connected the web, mobile, CLI, VS Code, Slack and Teams to a shared Copilot cloud session. It runs by default in a resumable, isolated sandbox and shares a compute allowance with GitHub Actions. The feature is live on paid plans, with cloud sandbox billing starting in November. Teams must manage both agent permissions and the shared budget.

October 30: OpenAI promises to support a common robots.txt signal for training preferences

OpenAI has announced that it will support the universal signals defined in IETF-related work for robots.txt. This allows websites to express whether certain content is allowed for basic generative AI training without the need to maintain separate crawling rules for each website. The implementation plan is still in the coming months. Existing OpenAI crawling controls remain in effect, and this is not yet a completed protocol switch.
