# Editorial review for issue 75

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: Data from Jump’s accounts in Hyperliquid shows that institutional trading concentration can be quantified. Aster changed RWA rewards to daily average OI. Uniswap redistributed some of the price differences resulting from stablecoin arbitrage operations to LP.

**What is the most important finding?**: Aster’s reward mechanism isn’t simply extending the settlement cycle. Instead, it shifts from hourly snapshots to a 24-hour average OI. This directly affects the relative returns of short-term accumulation and sustained positions.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The method of auditing account concentration has shifted from only auditing after settlement to auditing both account concentration and holding duration. Single-point snapshots cannot represent effective contributions independently.

**What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating transaction rewards, it’s possible to compare account concentration and average holdings, as well as checking display cycles, back-to-back settlements, and appeal rules.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If Aster’s subsequent data shows an increase in average OI, but there is no improvement in real transaction users, retention, or net liquidity, or if the proportion of concentrated accounts continues to rise, then the average reward quality does not improve.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Aegis AI’s regular IDOs have been deleted. Phoenix has added QQQ and SPY targets. Ethena has integrated TRON and OpenAI Data agents, but these efforts lack sufficient new mechanisms or incremental improvements.

### Six reader perspectives

1. Senior traders/Big whales: Whether Phoenix’s TWAP can reduce real slippage remains uncertain due to lack of execution data. It must be clear that this is merely a tool, and its functionality should not be presented as proven. Jump’s cumulative transactions cannot be directly equated with net risk and depth.
2. The product manager for competing products: Both Aster’s 24-hour average OI and Uniswap’s dynamic correction rates have clear mechanisms. However, Aster lacks sampling and rules for abnormal accounts. Uniswap is only available in two pools, so it cannot be directly copied.
3. CEO/Strategy: This update shifts from total traffic to traffic quality and value distribution. If only one decision can be made, then the time dimension of reward data and account concentration should be made auditable.
4. Skeptical reader: Launchpad daily token counts mix API-sampled lower bounds with complete onchain counts. SolvScore has only five registered wallets, so a new set of rules cannot be treated as evidence of adoption.
5. The editors: Jump, Aster, and Uniswap form a common framework. Regular ICOs, ordinary IDOs, and Data agents created yesterday have been removed to avoid filling up the categories and creating unnecessary value.
6. For new users: TWAP, average OI, and CBDC atomic settlements have been added as mechanisms and restrictions. Each numerical value is accompanied by reference or boundary values.
