Extended cuts the taker fee on real-world asset markets to one basis point

Event: Extended will reduce its RWA perpetual taker fee to one basis point from September 15. Makers continue to pay zero, with rebates from 0.2 to 1.0 basis points depending on their share of 30-day volume.

Mechanism: The taker fee is intended to attract active trading. The platform returns part of the revenue to market makers according to their share of continuous quotes, tying lower fees to order-book depth.

Second-order effects: Competition in RWA perpetuals is shifting from the number of assets listed to quoting costs and market-maker retention. There are no data yet showing that the fee advantage produces narrower spreads or sustained open interest.

Measurement limitsOne basis point is the stated taker fee. It excludes funding and slippage and does not account for maker rebates. The source provided neither the previous fee nor corresponding order-book depth.

Reya makes 12 markets reduce-only ahead of its order book migration

Event: Ahead of its V2 migration from an automated market maker to an order book, Reya placed 12 markets, including ADA and APT, in reduce-only mode. Traders may close or reduce a position, but cannot open or add to one.

Mechanism: The phased restrictions first prevent new risk, then move liquidity and matching to the new order book. Takers will pay three basis points and makers will pay zero.

Second-order effects: Reduce-only trading can reduce the disruption of a single forced settlement. A successful launch also requires market status, order permissions and the liquidity migration to agree. This adds to the market-lifecycle story tracked since August 28.

ScopeThis round affects 12 markets. The sources of these markets, the deadline for liquidity transfer, and the final handling rules for unliquidated positions remain undisclosed.
