Perp DEX

VALR integrates Hyperliquid execution into its licensed trading interface

Event: In a July 2 announcement, VALR said its Perps product would launch on the web on July 6, with mobile to follow. It added more than 200 markets covering stocks and indices including Tesla, Nvidia, Apple, the S&P 500 and SpaceX, as well as Brent and WTI crude, natural gas, gold, silver, major FX pairs and crypto. VALR described this as the first native integration of a Layer 1 protocol for execution and liquidity by a major regulated exchange. Hyperliquid held about $10.3 billion in open interest and recorded roughly $3.2 billion in 24-hour volume.

Mechanism: VALR retains the customer account, compliance relationship and interface. Customers open and close positions within VALR, while third-party liquidity providers on Hyperliquid handle order management, execution and liquidation. VALR’s terms disclaim responsibility for third-party errors.

Second-order effects: This was the fourth licensed integration tracked in two weeks, following Robinhood’s Arcus and Lighter deals and eToro’s tie-up with Extended. It extends the use of white-label perpetual infrastructure. Outsourcing execution leaves a separate question about customer recourse if the underlying protocol or matching service fails.

Bitcoin ETFs record eight consecutive weeks of net outflows while HYPE ETF inflows slow to a low

Event: The Block reported on July 4 that US spot bitcoin ETFs had $527 million in net outflows in the week ending July 2, their eighth straight week of withdrawals and longest such streak since launch. Year-to-date net outflows reached $5.53 billion. The three US spot HYPE ETFs, 21Shares' THYP, Bitwise's BHYP and Grayscale's HYPG, launched from mid-May. Combined weekly inflows slowed from a record $111 million to $4.32 million, the smallest so far, but remained positive for an eighth consecutive week.

Mechanism: HYPE ETFs manage a fraction of bitcoin ETF assets. The more meaningful comparison is the direction of flows in the same weak market: bitcoin funds saw continued net selling while HYPE funds still drew buyers, albeit at a slower pace.

Second-order effects: Some investors may be shifting from broad crypto exposure toward a specific combination of Hyperliquid's market position, token buybacks and HIP-3 expansion. With weekly HYPE ETF inflows at their lowest since launch, another week's flows could challenge that interpretation.
