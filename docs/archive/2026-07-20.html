Coinbase and Deribit put direct gateways into full production ahead of a planned migration of over 125 markets

Event: Coinbase International Exchange and Deribit put Direct Gateways into full production. Coinbase volume was scheduled to move to Deribit across more than 125 crypto, equity and commodity perpetuals. Full migration of positions and order books was planned for September 9. This was day 12 of coverage since July 9.

Mechanism: The integration brings order flow, risk management, portfolio margin and insurance funds into common matching and clearing infrastructure. New listings can draw on an established liquidity network.

Second-order effects: Shared infrastructure lowers the cost of adding markets. Exchanges competing on execution will need to demonstrate capital efficiency across assets, executable depth and continuity of liquidation in volatile markets.

ImplementationOnly the gateways were in full production. The account, position and order-book migration was scheduled for September 9, so shared liquidity across more than 125 markets had yet to be demonstrated.

Weekly theme | Regulated derivatives venues change margin infrastructure and the cost of holding perpetuals

Event: Marex completed its first regulated derivatives clearing transaction using USDC for initial margin. Coinbase provided qualified custody, instant fiat conversion and custom reporting. Separately, Pepperstone fixes perpetual funding rates one week ahead and accrues them daily as overnight swap charges.

Mechanism: These approaches bring round-the-clock collateral and peer-to-peer funding into traditional clearing and CFD accounts while retaining regulatory reporting, client asset segregation and existing risk controls. Institutions can use the features through their usual intermediaries.

Second-order effects: Traditional providers are adopting individual features of crypto perpetuals. Alongside round-the-clock trading and the absence of expiry, collateral mobility, predictable holding costs and auditable records are becoming important product benchmarks.

Measurement limitsUSDC was used in a first initial-margin transaction. Pepperstone commissioned the material on perpetual CFDs. Its claimed funding-cost advantage still needs independent execution data.

Uniswap directs v4 and Robinhood Chain fees toward UNI burns as its fee switch expands across networks

Uniswap put two proposals to final onchain votes. One would activate protocol fees for selected v4 pools across seven chains. The other would route v2 and v3 fees on Robinhood Chain into the UNI burn mechanism.

Dynamic v4 hook fees do not fit a single fixed setting for each pool. Governance must group pools by rule before calculating the protocol's share, turning multichain revenue allocation into an ongoing set of configurable fee rules.

ImplementationThe two final votes run from July 19 to July 26. The protocol fees and additional burns have yet to take effect. The proposals describe how they would work, but provide no realized revenue.
