JPMorgan says Hyperliquid receives 90% of the reserve interest on USDC held on its platform

Event: CoinDesk reported on July 14, citing JPMorgan, that Coinbase classifies USDC circulating on Hyperliquid as on-platform and passes 90% of the associated reserve interest to Hyperliquid. Coinbase and Circle had previously split the income roughly equally. Hyperliquid held about $6 billion in USDC, around 8% of supply. July volume exceeded $150 billion, 11.5% of Binance’s over the same period. The edition’s daily snapshot showed about $10 billion against $62.2 billion at Binance, or 16%, a different comparison window.

Mechanism: JPMorgan described a distribution agreement that reallocates USDC reserve income. It estimated that competition between Coinbase and Circle to retain Hyperliquid as a distribution channel would shift $60 million–$80 million in annual revenue from those companies to Hyperliquid.

Second-order effects: The deal provides a public example of a perpetual DEX securing a majority share of reserve interest tied to its own balances. Similar demands from large venues could reduce the income retained by the issuer and its partners, especially as interest rates fall. The scale of stablecoin balances gives venues a bargaining position beyond trading fees.

HIP-3 builder markets exceed native crypto volume for a day, with SK Hynix accounting for about half

Event: The Defiant cited July 14 Hyperliquid API data for day 34 of the series tracked since June 12. HIP-3 equity, commodity and index markets handled $5.41 billion of Tuesday's $10.44 billion, or 51.8%, surpassing native crypto perpetuals in the daily breakdown for the first time. Native markets recorded $5.03 billion, led by BTC at $2.69 billion and ETH at $1.27 billion. SK Hynix ADR perpetuals accounted for $1.62 billion of $3.2 billion in single-stock volume, about half.

Mechanism: HIP-3 also led on weekdays July 8, 9 and 10, but its share fell to 16–33% on July 5, 11 and 12, all weekend dates. The pattern suggests trading linked to traditional market hours, including institutional or arbitrage activity. TradeXYZ was the only deployer operating at material scale. One deployer and one stock account for much of the 51.8% result.

Second-order effects: Removing SK Hynix and the weekday effect weakens the claim that HIP-3 has overtaken native crypto. Aggregate figures for market breadth can conceal dependence on one asset and one deployer, a concentration risk highlighted in earlier coverage.

Aster falls by half despite buybacks as Lighter and pump.fun test similar supply mechanisms

Event: Aster updated its buyback figures on July 14, day 16 of coverage since June 30. From June 29 to July 13, it used 99% of daily fees to buy 3.0838 million ASTER and burned an equal team allocation, bringing burns since June 17 to 6.02 million. ASTER fell from around $1.26 in the June 17–29 round to $0.62, a decline of more than half. Staking APYs were reduced to 5.35% for a 26-week lock and a maximum 28.85% for 208 weeks. By comparison, Lighter completed its first onchain burn on July 11: 15.64 million repurchased LIT worth about $42 million, or 6.3% of circulating supply.

Mechanism: Aster, Lighter and Avantis link trading activity to supply reduction by using fees to repurchase and burn tokens. Aster still recorded about $1.65 billion in 24-hour perpetuals volume and $1.88 billion in open interest, placing it among large venues. Its buyback mechanism continued to receive fee revenue.

Second-order effects: Aster's buybacks and burns continued as its price halved, illustrating the limits of supply policy when liquidity preferences change. Pump.fun had a different result around its largest unlock on July 12, worth $130 million or 29% of circulating supply. More than $19 million in tokens had left team wallets by July 14 without a reported collapse. CoinGecko showed PUMP up 11% in 24 hours and 5.3% in seven days. That is a positive example under real selling pressure, though it does not establish that buybacks caused the gain.

Softer CPI cools expectations of a rate hike

June CPI comes in below expectations and bitcoin recovers above $64,000 as rate-hike odds fall

Event: CoinDesk and The Block reported on July 14 that June US CPI fell 0.4% month over month, the largest monthly decline since April 2020. Annual inflation was 3.5% versus 3.8% expected. Core CPI was flat on the month and up 2.6% on the year, also below expectations. The implied probability of a July rate hike fell from recent highs of 42–50%. BTC passed $63,000 within minutes and reached about $64,960, up 4.15% in 24 hours. ETH rose 6.2% and total crypto market capitalization gained 3.4%.

Mechanism: Fed officials' warnings about persistent inflation had lifted the implied probability of a July hike from 8% early in the month to 42–50%. Softer CPI weakened the case for near-term tightening, benefiting non-yielding assets such as bitcoin.
