Ostium reopens for risk reduction and liquidation at live prices before accepting new positions

Event: On July 23, after an eight-day pause, Ostium began a staged reopening. It first enabled closing positions, adding margin, cancelling or amending orders, take-profit and stop-loss execution, and liquidation. New positions were to follow after the backlog cleared. This was day 12 of coverage since July 15.

Mechanism: Existing positions and orders were preserved, but execution used live prices at reopening. Price movements during the halt did not trigger retroactive trades or liquidations. The sequence allowed existing exposures to be reduced before new risk entered.

Second-order effects: The process addressed position continuity after a halt. At reopening, the cause of the signing-key compromise and the terms of LP compensation remained unresolved.

ScopeSegregated trader margin was not stolen and became usable again. The affected LP funds remained unrecovered, and compensation had yet to be finalized.

CFTC extends energy perpetual consultation and distinguishes 24/7 trading from contracts without expiry

Event: On July 23, the CFTC extended its energy derivatives consultation by 30 days to August 26. New questions distinguish 24/7 trading of fixed-expiry futures from perpetual contracts on energy commodities that can be stored or physically delivered. This is day 38 of coverage since June 19.

Mechanism: Longer trading hours change when matching and clearing take place. An energy perpetual also changes expiry, delivery and the means by which futures prices converge with spot. The CFTC's distinction shows why continuous crypto trading alone does not establish that a perpetual is suitable for crude oil.

Second-order effects: US regulators are considering perpetuals asset by asset. Onchain energy venues seeking regulated access need evidence on weekend price formation, deliverable spot markets and safeguards against manipulation.

ImplementationThe regulator extended public consultation and added questions. It did not adopt a rule or approve a new energy perpetual.

OKX gives X-Perp a distant expiry date and launches the ZAMA contract

OKX launched ZAMAUSD X-Perp on July 24. It retains funding payments and round-the-clock trading but expires in about five years. As expiry approaches, a new longer-dated contract will trade alongside the old one so holders can roll their positions.

Settlement uses the average of index prices sampled every second over the final 30 minutes. This gives the contract a fixed point of convergence. Holders face funding and basis risk as well as the possibility of liquidity splitting during the roll. Here the “perp” label describes the funding mechanism despite a defined expiry.

ImplementationZAMA contracts are trading on the OKX website, app, and API. OKX has not disclosed open interest, depth, or use of rolls into more distant expiries.

Continuous pricing and physical delivery pose separate questions for energy perpetuals

Benefits: Around-the-clock commodity prices give traders a way to express weekend risk while traditional exchanges are closed.

Limitations: Physical oil markets depend on storage, delivery and regional supply. A continuously quoted index does not make the deliverable market continuously tradable.

Benefits: A distant expiry and final index settlement give the contract a defined endpoint and a way to force convergence.
