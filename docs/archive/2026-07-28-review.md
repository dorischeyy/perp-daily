# Editorial review

## A0 Reader value

1. **Why open this edition today?**: The two disclosures reveal the same blind spots from both sides. Ondo demonstrates how to use TEE and attestors to achieve private and high-performance execution. AFX proves that the development and product supply chain can lead to a situation where multiple signatures seem to fail simultaneously.
2. **What is the most important finding?**: Security boundaries are not just about contracts and final settlements; they also include where the code is built, who can approve deployments, who can reconstruct keys, and whether external observers can replay execution logs.
3. **Which prior assessment changed?**: Shift from “Offline matching is acceptable as long as the settlement on the chain is maintained” to “The construction of the chain, proof of the chain, and keychain must be auditable at the same time; only then can offline execution be considered a candidate.” Also, increase the risk weight for single enclaves and common trust domains.
4. **What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating offline matching, Keeper, or private position solutions, prioritize reproducible construction, independent attestors, key reconstruction thresholds, log replaying, and emergency revocations as key criteria for product and risk control.
5. **What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If Ondo achieves continuous real-time transaction and position data for 30 days, and provides independent attestors, hardware diversity, open log replaying, and on-chain status guarantees. Additionally, it must demonstrate that funds cannot be moved in case the chain is damaged or a single enclave fails. In that case, the current threshold should be lowered.
6. **Which news recap was removed?**: OKX has removed four new equity perpetual futures. HashKey has merged multiple regions into one entry point, and Payward has acquired embedded wallets. These are candidates that only provide facts about their launch, and cannot change the core conclusions of today’s situation.

### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: Ondo’s private execution can reduce exposure to positions, but the official documentation has not provided information on trading volume, OI, failover mechanisms, or verification of public logs. Therefore, the architectural promises cannot be equated with actual liquidity. AFX indicates that traders also need to consider whether the signature infrastructure shares the same supply chain.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: High performance and privacy are evolving from being “off-chain black boxes” to a competitive field where verifiable execution is key. The real difference lies not in which TEE is used, but in who approves the code, who stores the keys, and who can independently review the logs.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: Ondo’s decision to abandon the traditional L1 indicates that self-built consensus may not be the best way to allocate capital for derivatives infrastructure. However, having a single enclave and an unpublicized attestor also means that current deconfidence measures are not yet complete.
4. **Skeptics**: Ondo’s documentation does not contain independent operating data. AFX is also clearly not the final forensic report. What can be confirmed today are the architecture and main attack paths. However, it cannot be extrapolated to determine security, recovery results, or compensation.
5. **Editor**: Both approaches revolve around “the true trust boundaries of off-chain execution.” One approach proposes a new architecture, while the other provides examples of failures. Neither approach uses conventional tokens, financing, or market-based mechanisms.
6. **Newcomers**: The text explains why TEE, attestors, product libraries, and multi-signatures are related, and defines terms such as matching, margin, deployment, and withdrawal permissions.
