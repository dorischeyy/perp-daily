Binance includes ETFs and single stocks in its round-the-clock perpetual offerings

Event: On October 12, Binance listed four USDT perpetuals: ZCSH, MSTU, SNPS and RDW. They cover a Zcash ETF, a leveraged ETF targeting twice MSTR’s daily return and two U.S. stocks. All trade 24/7 with up to 20x leverage and eight-hour funding.

Mechanism: ZCSH and MSTU layer ETF fees, rebalancing and tracking error on top of the perpetual index and funding rate. Trading continues when the underlying securities are closed. The contract framework must therefore account for equity price discovery, ETF path dependence and round-the-clock margin risk.

ImplementationFour contracts have been launched, but the announcement does not disclose trading volumes, OI, price differences, or the prices of underlying securities during market closures.

Event: PerpFinder scanned the order book on October 12th at 23:57 UTC. Paradex became the lowest-cost place to place a perpetual order for 100,000 USD BTC, at a price of 0.3 basis points. Australia proposes applying the same protections to crypto-settled CFDs

Event: The ASIC proposed extending the retail protection order for CFDs by ten years, and included CFDs that are settled through digital asset transfers in the definition. The proposal still requires leverage limits, standardized liquidations, and negative balance protection. It also specifies that opposite positions cannot reduce the initial margin for a single CFD.

Mechanism: The proposal applies comparable safeguards to leveraged exposures whether they settle in cash, through offsetting positions or in digital assets. The four-week consultation runs until November 9. Extending the order still requires ministerial approval.

ScopeASIC states that after six months of implementing current rules, retail account net losses have decreased by 91%. The new scope and ten-year extension have not yet taken effect officially.

AlloX offers a valuation discount on Sonar in exchange for a longer lockup

AlloX launched a community sales campaign worth 1 million USD on October 12th, accepting USDC and USDT on Ethereum. It used a three-tier scheme to reduce the fully diluted valuation from 65 million USD to 39 million USD.

Silver offers full unlocking during the TGE period. Gold offers 20% unlocking with a 12-month linear release, while Platinum offers a three-month cliff period followed by a 12-month release period, resulting in the lowest valuation. Excess funds are distributed proportionally and returned when settled.

ImplementationThe commitment window has been extended until October 19th. The TGE and distribution dates remain unconfirmed. 1 million USD is the fundraising target, not the amount already raised.

Standard Chartered launches RLUSD custody

Standard Chartered’s DIFC branch opened custody for XRPL-issued RLUSD to eligible institutional clients. It is working with Ripple and LMAX Group to make RLUSD eligible collateral.

The planned collateralized mirror will keep RLUSD under bank custody. LMAX will be responsible for recognizing its value for trading and collateral management purposes, reducing the need for direct transfers to trading platforms. This capability still requires completing documents, testing, internal approvals, and regulatory preparations.

ImplementationThe hosting service has been launched. LMAX collateral qualifications and mirroring capabilities are still in planning stage. There is no indication of the size of the collateral or the launch date.

FSB identifies cross-border coordination as a gap in stablecoin oversight

FSB submitted a global stablecoin cross-border vulnerabilities report to G20, indicating that the same arrangement can span multiple jurisdictions, entity types, and regulatory authorities. It’s difficult to fully assess risk propagation in a single region.

The next step is to improve cross-border monitoring, information sharing, and collaboration for the implementation of recommendations in 2023. It’s important to enhance the coverage and comparability of existing data sources. This is a document for monitoring and policy coordination, not a new set of rules for stablecoins.

ScopeThe document addresses arrangements for stablecoins that may cross borders and are systemically important. This does not mean that all stablecoins in a single region are considered systemic risks.
