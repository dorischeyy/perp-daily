Robinhood extends European retail perpetual futures to commodities, ETFs and foreign exchange

Event: On July 2, Robinhood announced perpetual futures on gold, silver, crude oil, ETFs and EUR/USD for European investors. The products offer up to 10× leverage and 24/7 trading. The same announcement included Robinhood Earn, targeting about 7% annualized on dollar stablecoins, and plans to expand crypto services in Canada, Singapore and the United Kingdom.

Mechanism: These perpetuals sit within the licensed brokerage product line. The Arcus and Lighter integrations on Robinhood Chain are a separate onchain distribution route. Both extend leveraged access across asset classes.

Second-order effects: This was the fourth development tracked since July 2. Taken together, the licensed offering and the Arcus and Lighter integrations show that Robinhood is pursuing two distribution routes for perpetuals.

Kraken accepts tokenized U.S. stocks and ETFs as collateral for leveraged trading

Event: On July 3, Kraken made 10 tokenized US stocks and ETFs, including AAPL, NVDA, TSLA, MSTR, GLD and CRCL xStocks, eligible as futures and margin collateral. Haircuts range from 10% for broad-market ETFs to 30% for volatile stocks such as MSTR and HOOD. Collateral caps are up to $1 million for broad ETFs and $250,000 for most stocks. Futures collateral is available outside the US, including the EEA. Margin trading collateral excludes the EEA.

Mechanism: Holders can use a tokenized stock to secure a leveraged position while retaining their exposure to the underlying shares. This reduces the need to maintain separate investment holdings and trading collateral.

Second-order effects: Kraken's centralized offering and Grvt's July 3 unified margin initiative both let the same asset serve more than one purpose. For perpetuals platforms, collateral efficiency can influence retention alongside the number of markets listed and the depth of their order books.

ESMA treats prediction-market event contracts as restricted binary options

Event: On July 4, the European Securities and Markets Authority said sports and political event contracts on platforms such as Kalshi and Polymarket fall within the EU's retail binary-options restrictions when their payouts have the structure of a derivative. National gambling rules may also apply, and tokenized contracts may raise additional questions under MiCA.

Mechanism: ESMA emphasized the substance of the payoff. Describing a product as a prediction market does not exempt it from derivatives rules. The question parallels the dispute in CME's US lawsuit over whether perpetuals are swaps or futures.

Second-order effects: A July 1 analysis noted that MiCA had tightened access for unlicensed spot exchanges while oversight of perpetuals, estimated at about 80% of crypto volume, remained uneven. European retail users could access up to 50x leverage on Hyperliquid and 200x on Aster. ESMA's position addressed prediction markets. The treatment of perpetuals remained a central question as Robinhood expanded its European retail offering.
