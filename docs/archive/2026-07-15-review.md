### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: Two things can be determined today: First, Hyperliquid has opened up new revenue streams beyond transaction fees, thanks to its USDC reserves, indicating that its cash flow resilience is stronger than just looking at transaction volume. Second, Aster’s continuous repurchase efforts have halved its coin price. However, the narrative of “repurchase and destruction” should not be used as a reason to buy. It’s necessary to evaluate the price drivers separately. HIP-3’s “51.8%” is a noise-level headline. Breaking it down, it’s just SK Hynix as a target, along with the effect of working days, which doesn’t constitute a new investment judgment.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: Hyperliquid takes 90% of USDC reserves from Coinbase as a way to monetize “deposited funds”. We currently have no corresponding actions in this regard. HIP-3’s “breadth” is actually a false appearance of “insufficient depth”. What should be done is to focus on “comprehensively managing 1-2 high-topic assets” rather than “listing more assets”.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: This week’s main topic is “The monetization methods of perp platforms are shifting from single-fee models to stablecoin revenue sharing.” This is a crucial opportunity for us. If we have to make a decision, it should be whether we can negotiate similar revenue-sharing terms with the issuers/host providers regarding the USDT assets held within the platform.
4. **Skeptics**: JPMorgan's "11.5%" refers to the monthly average, while our "16%" is calculated based on a cross-verification of a single-day snapshot in the docs/market.json file. These two figures have different scopes of measurement. The report clearly states that "single-day data itself is subject to fluctuations, but the magnitude and direction are consistent." Therefore, the two figures with different scopes are not directly equated. Pump.fun's "No collapse after unlocking" data is only available for a short period of 3 days (unlocked from July 12 to July 15). Since the sample size is still small, the report only states that "no collapse has occurred so far," leaving room for further analysis.
5. **Editor**: In the lead section, only one thing was mentioned (the distribution of USDC reserves). There was no stacking of information. Both HIP-3 and Aster are continuations of older lines of reporting. The days are marked using inline callbacks, so the old content isn’t repeated from the beginning. There’s room for improvement: the CPI entry for the Crypto section has a long paragraph. However, since it’s the most significant macro-pricing factor for that day, and it doesn’t overlap with other entries, it’s kept intact.
6. **Newcomers**: Terms like “HIP-3”, “ZFP”, “reserve revenue sharing”, and “prisoner’s dilemma” are explained in detail in the text. The mechanisms behind these terms are explained in the “events/mechanisms” section. No jargon is left unexplainated.
