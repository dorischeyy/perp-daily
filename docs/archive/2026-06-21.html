Exchanges compete over how trading fees benefit tokenholders

Weekly trend | Aster launches its “198%” buyback and burn, earmarking 99% of daily fees for token purchases

Event: On June 17, Aster announced it would use 99% of daily trading fees to buy ASTER on the open market through time-weighted purchases and burn an equivalent amount from team reserves. Aster calls this its “198%” program. Its long-term target is to reduce supply from 8 billion tokens to 3 billion. ASTER rose about 23% to $0.79 that day before giving back some gains. Aster was processing roughly $1.75 billion in daily volume, with $1.91 billion in open interest.

Mechanism: Purchases spread over time can limit immediate price impact. Burning team reserves reduces potential insider supply. Holders of veASTER may benefit indirectly, but the arrangement depends on genuine fee income. Reported volume alone cannot establish that the program is sustainable.

Second-order effects: GMX v1 and v2 had already generated more than $100 million in annualized fees. Aster’s program extends the use of fee income to support a perpetual exchange’s token. It gives analysts a reason to examine verifiable revenue alongside TVL and reported volume, particularly where trading activity may be artificial.

Speculative trading shifts from memecoins toward perpetuals

Launchpad

Weekly theme | Pump.fun activity falls 80% in three months as daily revenue drops from $4.8 million to $800,000

Event: The Block reported on June 16 that pump.fun’s seven-day average token graduation rate had fallen 80% in three months to 0.26%. Daily revenue averaged about $800,000 in June, down 83% from $4.8 million six months earlier. Its daily contribution to Solana fees fell from 33,000 SOL in January to 5,300 SOL, down 84%. The report said some capital that once traded Solana meme tokens had moved to perpetuals, particularly on Hyperliquid. This follows the June 20 edition’s coverage of GO.

Mechanism: Hyperliquid launched permissionless HIP-3 markets in October 2025. They accounted for 35% of its volume and had reached $1.43 billion in open interest at their peak. Crypto, stock and commodity exposures offer leveraged speculation with deeper liquidity and without relying on a new token issuer to maintain a market. Traders can take positions directly rather than waiting for a launchpad token to graduate.

Second-order effects: The figures suggest a shift in retail instruments as well as a decline in launchpad activity. Pump.fun’s GO bounty platform, which attracted New York legislative attention, attempts to monetize promotion as issuance falls. Solana’s decline in pump.fun fees also raises the question of whether venues such as Jupiter Perps can replace some of that revenue.

BITA shows how brokerage demand for bitcoin exposure can extend to income strategies.
