# Editorial review

## A0 Reader value

1. **Why open this edition today?**: When transactions at the four major perpetual markets decreased by 20.3%, the OI only decreased by 2.6%. At the same time, perpetual securities were extended to leveraged ETFs. Both market turnover and product complexity showed changes that warrant revaluation.
2. **What is the most important finding?**: Hyperliquid’s OI decreased by only 0.5%, while trading volume decreased by 27.6%. This indicates that maintaining positions does not necessarily mean stabilizing the exit strategy. The clearing capacity under low turnover rates requires further observation.
3. **Which prior assessment changed?**: The approach to integrating AI agents into trading interfaces is considered as a means of distribution. Instead, it’s recommended to separate read-only and execution permissions, and to set up independent risk budgets for each agent.
4. **What decision can readers make?**: If the product team verifies agency trading, they should first use read-only data. During the execution phase, market conditions, order types, and nominal values should be limited. Keeper or liquidation permissions should not be granted to general agents.
5. **What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If subsequent data shows that transactions and OI recover simultaneously, and if the depth of transactions increases and the liquidation slippage decreases, then the conclusion that “low turnover amplifies risk of exit” needs to be re-evaluated. If independent evaluations show that execution agents can continue to operate without any hard limits, it is necessary to re-evaluate the limits on their permissions.
6. **Which news recap was removed?**: The Beldex financing release, Sherlock’s similar AI audits, fluctuations in the general coin price, and old regulatory reviews without new procedural documents have been deleted.

### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: The stability of Hyperliquid’s OI does not mean that its liquidity is stable. The text retains details such as volume, depth, funding rates, and the discrepancy in settlement distributions.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: OKX’s launch of SOXSUSD reveals the combination of underlying leveraged ETFs and contract leverage. Margin requirements and suitability cannot be applied to ordinary perpetual stocks.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: Ondo’s cumulative nominal trading volume demonstrates its expansion speed, but it cannot replace continuous OI, net revenue, or user rights. Avoid directly including size-related details in the roadmap.
4. **Skeptics**: The capabilities and indicators of MaxQ, Math Magic, KTX, and Prem AI are mainly based on company announcements. The text clearly distinguishes between financing, product launches, adoption, and independent verification.
5. **Editor**: The four Perp scenarios each address overall turnover, Hyperliquidness differentiation, complex securities targets, and regulatory access. They are not repeated with the same conclusion.
6. **Newcomers**: Each key figure is accompanied by a time frame, reference, and restrictions. There is no mixing of cumulative financing, TVL, cumulative transactions, and actual revenue.

### Ten-dimension assessment

- Signal-to-noise ratio 9: Remove ordinary market data and duplicate security audit reports. Only events that involve structural changes are retained.
- Ranking 9: First, write about perpetual transactions and product risks. Then, discuss financing, institutional infrastructure, and AI execution capabilities.
- Insight vs. Handling: OI and transactions are separated into exit arrangements, while agency interfaces are separated into permissions and risk budgets.
- Depth: All four aspects of Perp DEX use a three-part structure involving events, mechanisms, and secondary effects.
- Actionability 9: Product judgments are based on read-only processes, hard limits, manual confirmation, cancellation options, and attribution logs.
- Accurate tracing: Remote market blobs, previous values, source dates, and storylines have all been fully traced.
- Narrative coherence: 9: Predictions about the market and TradFi/RWA are presented in a coherent manner within the text. There is no repetition in the top-level progress blocks.
- Readability: Key ratios are presented with one decimal place; complex regulatory and agency procedures are simplified to a single level.
- House View 9: Company objectives, financing, cumulative transactions, and regulatory statements have not been written as either adoption, revenue, or formal approvals.
- Clarity: Four news sections are maintained with 3 to 4 items each. Product-related decisions are only recorded if they truly change the boundaries of entry.

### Top three revisions

1. The draft describes Hyperliquid’s OI as resilient, but it includes scenarios where trading volume decreases. The ratio of OI to trading volume becomes slower over time, requiring observation of liquidation capacity.
2. The initial draft treats SOXSUSD as a regular equity perpetual futures, with additional leverage for ETFs. It also addresses daily resets, path dependencies, and secondary leverage risks.
3. The initial draft describes KTX’s proxy execution as channel expansion. It includes requirements for permission isolation, hard limits, revocation, and auditing before forming a product judgment.
