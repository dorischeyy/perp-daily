Oracle security and the financing of new HIP-3 markets

Perp DEX

Ostium discloses unauthorized oracle signing and pauses trading

Event: Ostium, an Arbitrum exchange for RWA and FX perpetuals previously profiled for a 400-day EUR/USD long, reported an incident on July 15. Estimated losses from the OLP liquidity vault were $18–23.7 million in USDC. The vault fell from about $32.7 million to around $9 million, a loss of roughly 72% of TVL. Ostium suspended trading and said it was investigating. It had yet to publish an incident report or compensation plan.

Mechanism: The incident involved the reliability of Ostium's oracle authorization system. Third-party security firm Blockaid detected and reported the anomaly. The protocol's own controls did not prevent the losses.

Implications: CoinDesk counted this as the third major incident involving privileged automation infrastructure in 2026, after a roughly $6 million incident at Summer.fi a week earlier. Permission management for price infrastructure is an important dependency for perpetuals exchanges, particularly as they expand into FX, commodities and stocks that rely on offchain prices.

A Hyperliquid treasury company offers HYPE stake to market builders, introducing an intermediary for HIP-3 entry

Event: On July 15, Nasdaq-listed Hyperliquid treasury company Hyperion DeFi announced a HYPE Asset Use Service (HAUS) agreement with Skew Technologies. Hyperion will lease 500,000 HYPE worth about $33.6 million to satisfy Skew's HIP-3 deployer staking requirement, in exchange for equity and a share of market services revenue. Skew plans to launch institutional perpetuals on HIP-3. This is day 34 of coverage since June 12.

Mechanism: HIP-3 generally requires a deployer to stake at least 500,000 HYPE to open markets. Hyperion can lease its treasury holdings to teams that have the expertise to run a venue but lack the required tokens, creating an intermediary for the staking capital.

Second-order effects: A deployer can now secure backing from a HYPE holder instead of accumulating the full stake itself. This could support new entrants while giving major holders greater influence over which venues launch. Access to staking capital joins settlement assets, distribution and order flow as a source of competitive advantage.

Robinhood Chain launchpad Noxa waives its commission before closure, directing 100% of revenue to creators

On day 14 of coverage since July 2, Noxa, Robinhood Chain's largest token launchpad, had earned nearly $12 million in fees in one week, driven by the memecoin CASHCAT. It stopped accepting new launches on July 11, took its website offline on July 13 and announced on July 15 that it would return 100% of trading revenue to creators. CASHCAT fell more than 33% in 24 hours after the announcement.

Noxa gave up its own trading fee income. Meanwhile, Robinhood Chain's 35 promised RWA perpetuals markets had remained behind a waiting list for 14 days since July 1. Memecoin activity, the source of much of the chain's early usage, also faced questions about its sustainability.

Pump.fun’s largest unlock delivers $86.5 million, below expectations, as the token rises 13%

On day 16 of the fee-funded buybacks series tracked since June 30, pump.fun's July 15 transfers totaled 57.279 billion PUMP, about $86.5 million across 121 wallets. Its largest team and investor unlock had been forecast at about $127 million, or 29% of circulating supply. Actual transfers fell below that calendar estimate and marked the start of three-year vesting. PUMP rose 13% over the next 24 hours.

Onchain analysts noted that unlock calendars track eligibility for distribution, which can precede actual wallet transfers. The market may already have priced in a larger worst-case release. The positive price response provides a clearer test of how a token with a buyback and burn program behaves around an actual unlock.

Japan’s parliament passes legislation bringing crypto under financial instruments law and setting a 20% tax rate

Japan's legislature passed a bill on July 15 placing crypto assets including bitcoin, ether and XRP under the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act from fiscal 2027. A flat 20% tax on crypto income is scheduled to replace a top rate of 55% from 2028. The legislation provides a legal basis for Japan's first spot crypto ETFs. A Tokyo Stock Exchange representative expected trading could begin as early as 2027.

The framework pairs stricter insider-trading and disclosure rules with lower taxes and a potential ETF route. That combination could reduce the cost of regulated participation for Japanese retail investors.
