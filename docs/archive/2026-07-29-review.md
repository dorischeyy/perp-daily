5. **What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: Reconsider if at least three cross-market IPO perpetuals sustain spreads below 3% before the opening, avoid abnormal liquidations or price dislocations for 24 hours after transition, and retain independent market-maker depth without cross-border spot arbitrage.
6. **Which news recap was removed?**: KuCoin’s single contract funding payment intervals, Payward’s wallet asset acquisition, and the temporary suspension of the Minnesota prediction market ban are candidates that do not change the product’s current conclusions.

### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: The initial success of CXMT does not guarantee smooth trading in subsequent periods. Foreign users lack a way to hedge their positions. Funding payments, price discrepancies, and the exit of a single market maker can still pose independent risks.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: The real challenge in the pre-market situation isn’t about launching the product in advance, but rather how to switch the index, handle suspension issues, and coordinate company actions during the listing process. It’s also important to ensure that the gap created at the opening of the market doesn’t lead to a cascading liquidation process.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: RWA competition is creating two types of defenses: one involves cross-market price switching, while the other involves a fully regulated blockchain for spot tokens. Simply expanding the list of targets cannot replace these two types of capabilities.
4. **Skeptics**: CXMT has only one event sample, and only 6.73% of its capital is tradable on the first day. Securitize has obtained licenses, but no additional asset size or trading adoption has been reported, so it cannot be extrapolated as a commercial success.
5. **Editor**: The two approaches each address how prices enter the real market and how rights are transferred by real institutions. The execution safety lines from yesterday’s Ondo and AFX have not been repeated.
6. **Newcomers**: The text explains what pre-market perpetual futures, spot arbitrage, ATS, and transfer agents do. It also clarifies that tokenized stocks do not automatically equal real stock rights.
