# Editorial review

## A0 Reader value

1. **Why open this edition today?**: Equity perpetual futures continue to expand into the long-tail industries. However, the structure of Hyperliquid and Binance’s OI/transaction volume, MEV research, and proxy incidents indicate that the speed of expansion ultimately determines product quality, with pricing and control mechanisms playing a key role.
2. **What is the most important finding?**: Hyperliquid’s OI/day transaction ratio is about 1.52, which is nearly three times that of Binance’s ratio of 0.52. This means that the risk density carried by the on-chain platform is higher. Competitiveness cannot be judged solely by transaction volume.
3. **Which prior assessment changed?**: Replace the goal of filling gaps in leading equity perpetuals with a listing requirement: verify the index source, closed-market pricing, corporate actions and handling of disruptions for each asset.
4. **What decision can readers make?**: Product resources should be prioritized for building asset-level gateways and monitoring asset risks. Do not lower pricing and risk control requirements just because competitors add five new assets at once.
5. **What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If long-term equity perpetual futures maintain low price differences, low abnormal settlements, and high retention rates despite lack of asset-level rules, or if an increase in OI/trade volume doesn’t lead to higher losses, then the priority of control should be lowered.
6. **Which news recap was removed?**: The quarterly reports of Circle, old tests of AISI, PROVE unlocking, and general cooperation announcements have been deleted to avoid duplicating yesterday’s content or simply copying the list.

2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: Bitget's five new sector listings show how quickly competitors can expand, but the announcement did not establish depth, open interest or spreads during cash-market closures. The assessment does not use listing count alone.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: Long-tail stocks, cross-chain transactions, and proxy networks all indicate a priority for infrastructure investments. First, focus on reusable control mechanisms, then expand assets and automate permissions.
4. **Skeptics**: All three studies are clearly papers or reviews, and the models, benchmarks, and theoretical auctions are not presented as production results. The Meta incident is also attributed to confirmed configuration errors.
5. **Editor**: Four columns, three entries each. The entries for Circle, AISI, and PROVE, which were written yesterday, have been deleted. Only one judgment that will change the product ranking remains.
6. **Newcomers**: Each important number has a label, with unlocking, TGE, test networks, papers, and real production data presented separately.
