# Editorial review

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: FinCEN has taken formal measures to cut off MEXC’s financial channels with US financial institutions. However, this measure will only take effect after 30 days. The risks related to trading availability and settlement must be evaluated separately.

**What is the most important finding?**: It is recommended to prioritize MEXC in the instructions, as this is a formal order that can be changed based on the expectations of institutional transactions and user withdrawals. The basis for this is the FinCEN order and MEXC’s response. The boundary is not yet effective, but it has a higher priority than the progress of tokenized stocks this week, as well as the research on the old GMX mechanism.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The risk assessment of exchange compliance has been upgraded from regional licensing restrictions to a requirement that U.S. financial institutions must not handle related fund transfers. The risk priority of exit paths has been significantly increased.

**What decision can readers make?**: Products and risk management should prioritize verifying the platform’s trading status, fiat currency channels, institutional settlements, and user withdrawals separately. The monitoring of exit paths should not be relaxed just because MEXC is still operating normally.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If FinCEN revokes its orders, or if the Federal Register does not publish any information, or if MEXC still has verified US bank settlement channels after the rule takes effect, and if the data for deposits and withdrawals does not deteriorate, the risk assessment should be lowered.

**Which news recap was removed?**: MEXC’s Dutch business has deleted the new single announcement regarding new registrations, as it exceeds the 72-hour limit and does not add any new evidence to the judgment regarding today’s funding channels.

In the past seven issues, there were no studies focused on the complete risk chain of GMX. This issue introduces a new concept: traders who are forced to close their positions, as well as their ADL and LP exits, are placed in the same pool for settlement logic. Information regarding the actual execution success rate of related orders before forced closure, historical ADL data for each market, and the frequency and duration of LP redemption failures will be investigated.
