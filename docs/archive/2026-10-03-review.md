# Editorial review for issue 95

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: Velocity made future recoveries from the security incident tradable. CME withdrew its proposed 24/7 small crude oil futures. The two developments expose distinct questions for derivatives venues: compensation after losses and the risk limits of continuous trading.

**What is the most important finding?**: CME paused the product after the industry raised concerns about unintended risks. Regulated venues competing with onchain perpetuals still need rules for pricing during market closures, liquidity and responsibility.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The market added evidence relevant to the U.S. legal classification of perpetual futures. CME withdrew its proposal for small-sized, continuously traded crude oil futures after the CFTC had halted it. The withdrawal did not resolve the merits of the litigation over whether perpetuals are swaps or futures.

**What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating continuous trading or compensation mechanisms, it is important to verify product availability, funds availability, redemption ratios, and extreme risk control separately. The announcement of launch or partnership commitments should not be equated with actual capacity.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If the Velocity compensation pool reaches over $100 million within 30 days, and the actual payments exceed 50% of the losses, or if CME restarts with a new structure and there are no significant issues for 30 consecutive days, then the assumptions regarding stress from phased repayments and prudent trading practices need to be adjusted.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Announcements that only mention standard token issuance, lack verifiable release dates, and do not specify operational boundaries have been deleted. There are also old news items without any new mechanisms or new states.

### Six reader perspectives

1. Trader: OKX confirmed contract specifications and the funding schedule but gave no depth, open interest or data on price divergence while the underlying stock market is closed. Listing count cannot establish execution quality.
2. Product managers of competing products: Velocity, Pump, and Umia are all writing revenue or future cash flows into verifiable rules. However, whether the source of funds, order of arrival, and mechanisms for handling failures are truly effective remains to be seen.
3. CEO strategy: Both CME’s withdrawal of products and Blast’s shutdown indicate that if the infrastructure cannot cover ongoing costs and risk responsibilities, scale does not automatically become sustainable business.
4. Risk control and compliance: CME took the initiative to withdraw its offerings, while the SEC remains a proposal. BUDA still shows Pre-Launch status. The text does not describe suspension, proposals, or plans as regulatory conclusions or completion results.
5. Skeptics: The Velocity cooperation commitments, Anthropic training objectives, and OpenAI’s cost reduction efforts all come from disclosures by the main entities. The context is limited to objectives, limits, or unmet funds.
6. For new users: Each item describes operations that are already in progress, proposed rules, operations that have been stopped, and items waiting to be auctioned. This prevents mixing different states into one description.
