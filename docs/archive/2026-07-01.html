Weekly theme | HIP-3 builders exit as Phantom hires Ventuals founders and Dreamcash closes USDT markets

Event: Phantom hired Ventuals’ three founders for its trading and data team. Ventuals, which had listed markets on private companies including OpenAI and Anthropic, had announced its closure earlier in the month. Tether-linked Dreamcash separately announced that it would settle and close all CASH-series HIP-3 markets in three batches between June 30 and July 2.

Mechanism: Both projects staked HYPE to list markets under HIP-3. Phantom absorbed the team behind Ventuals into a wallet distribution business. Dreamcash cited a different problem: after native USDC integration on Hyperliquid, the user experience of a USDT-denominated venue had become too difficult to sustain.

Second-order effects: Permissionless listing leaves builders dependent on the venue’s settlement standard and on access to order flow. These exits shift the focus of the HIP-3 coverage, begun on June 12, from expansion toward consolidation.

Lighter plans to burn LIT bought back with 100% of fees and targets a 6% staking yield

Event: Lighter, a newer perpetuals DEX known for zero fees and a points program, said it had used 100% of trading fee revenue since the token generation event to buy about 15.5 million LIT, roughly 6.3% of circulating supply. Future repurchases would be burned permanently, with the first burn scheduled for several weeks after Q2 2026. Ecosystem reserves would replace pre-launch revenue subsidies for staking, targeting an initial 6% annualized yield.

Mechanism: Burning repurchases ties fee income to a permanent reduction in supply. Paying staking rewards from ecosystem reserves separates the source of those incentives from the buyback budget. The team acknowledged that its previous subsidy model was unsustainable.

Second-order effects: Following Aster's allocation of 99% of fees to its buyback and burn program, Lighter also opted for burns. It is the second venue in the fee-funded supply reduction trend tracked since June 30, as newer exchanges seek to support valuations amid falling volume.

Launchpad

Pump.fun removes its Tokenized Agent launch route, citing excessive competition among launch channels

Event: On June 30, pump.fun removed the Tokenized Agent issuance option introduced on March 13, 2026, about three and a half months earlier. The change applies only to new issues. Existing tokens are unaffected. The team described it as the first step in simplifying the platform.

Mechanism and second-order effects: Tokenized Agent let issuers attach an AI agent to a token and use the agent's revenue to buy back and burn it. The team said multiple competing issuance options had encouraged a damaging player-versus-player dynamic. The decision narrows the launchpad's offerings as demand for memecoins weakens.

Stablecoin and ETF rules

Crypto

More than 140 firms including Stripe, Coinbase and BlackRock form Open USD as Circle falls over 17% in a day

Event: Open Standard announced the launch of Open USD, a stablecoin network competing with Circle's USDC. More than 140 founding partners included Stripe, Coinbase, Mastercard, Visa, BlackRock, BNY, Google, Solana and Aave. Circle shares (CRCL) fell more than 17% that day to close below $63, down roughly 55% from mid-May.

Mechanism: Open USD proposes fee-free minting and redemption for businesses and a share of reserve income for partners, similar to the Paxos-led Global Dollar Network (USDG). Coinbase, a major USDC distributor that already shares in USDC reserve income, was also listed as a founding partner of the competing network.

Second-order effects: Competing stablecoin alliances could weaken USDC's distribution advantage and change how issuers share reserve income. For perpetual exchanges, the practical questions are which stablecoins they can accept for collateral and settlement and how competition affects fees.

SEC seeks comment on automatic listing as it reviews the approval framework for novel ETFs

Event: The US SEC announced a review of its approach to newer ETFs, including crypto products and funds linked to prediction markets. It invited public comment on the generic listing standards under which qualifying funds can list and become effective automatically.

Second-order effects: The proliferation of crypto and prediction-market ETF proposals has exposed gaps in the generic listing standards. A review could affect the timing of new altcoin and prediction-market ETFs, just as existing funds face persistent net outflows.
