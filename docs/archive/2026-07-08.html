Ondo Perps launches with tokenized stocks eligible as margin

Event: On July 7, Ondo Finance launched Ondo Perps through Ondo Global Panama. The platform offered eight markets: SPCX, MU, AAPL, NVDA, TSLA, QQQ, gold and silver. It announced 24/7 trading and leverage of up to 20×. The United States and Panama were among the excluded jurisdictions. The first-week USDC rewards pool was $150,000, against a planned $3 million total. Actual adoption had yet to be established.

Mechanism: Traders can pledge eligible tokenized stocks and ETFs they already own without first selling them for stablecoins. Both tokenized securities and stablecoins can serve as collateral for a separate leveraged position.

Second-order effects: The design tests whether tokenized assets can function as collateral across markets. Meaningful usage could influence how competing real-world asset venues design margin. First-day availability alone provides little evidence about adoption.

Weekly theme | Regulated venues pursue permissions spanning crypto, equities and derivatives

Event: At least three large regulated platforms expanded their brokerage or derivatives offerings within a week. On July 7, Coinbase obtained a UK FCA MiFID investment services authorization for perpetual futures on crypto, equities and commodities for institutional and advanced traders. UK retail users also gained access to spot equities on Coinbase for the first time. The same day, Gemini launched commission-free stock trading in most US states. Its CEO said the aim was a regulated platform combining crypto, stocks and derivatives. On July 2, eToro led an investment in Extended and announced plans for a Zengo wallet integration.

Mechanism: Coinbase secured a license, Gemini introduced stock brokerage and eToro invested in an onchain exchange. Each aims to give customers a single regulated point of access to crypto, stocks and derivatives.

Second-order effects: As established regulated exchanges offer perpetuals, onchain venues will need to compete on specific advantages such as permissionless listings, fees, leverage and market breadth. The race to list new asset classes now includes large regulated exchanges as well as other protocols.
