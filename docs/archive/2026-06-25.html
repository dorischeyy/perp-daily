Weekly theme | Hyperliquid HIP-3 adds a perpetual on Strategy’s discounted STRC preferred shares

Event: On June 23, trade.xyz listed a perpetual on Strategy’s variable-rate Series A preferred stock, STRC, through HIP-3 with up to 10× leverage. STRC was trading at $82–88 against a $100 face value, attracting arbitrage activity. Equity-linked markets accounted for 23 of Hyperliquid’s 30 largest pairs by open interest, and HIP-3 open interest exceeded $3 billion.

Mechanism: HIP-3 builders can deploy perpetual markets and backstop liquidity without individual listing approval from Hyperliquid. Trade.xyz had already listed SpaceX, which traded $1.4 billion on its June 12 IPO day, the S&P 500 and large technology stocks. STRC extends its business from pre-IPO price discovery to trading the premium or discount on a listed security.

Second-order effects: Binance, Bybit and Bitget temporarily withdrew products on SpaceX’s listing day when they could not source the underlying inventory. Hyperliquid’s onchain market continued operating. As equity perpetual ranges broaden, venue competition is likely to turn on both listing speed and executable liquidity.

Lighter's daily trader count halves in a week to 7,972 as Upbit listing momentum fades in three days and volume falls from $1.7 billion to $530 million

Event: Upbit, a leading South Korean exchange, listed Lighter's LIT on June 19. By June 23, daily active traders had roughly halved from 16,000 to 7,972, and 24-hour volume had dropped from a peak of $1.7 billion to $531 million. LIT fell 11% that day and broke below the $1.60 neckline of a head-and-shoulders pattern.

Mechanism: Lighter promotes its zero-fee order book built with zero-knowledge technology. Upbit's listing raised LIT's visibility and made the token easier to sell, but the initial traffic faded quickly. Trading a token on a Korean exchange and returning to trade derivatives on its DEX are distinct decisions.

Second-order effects: Lighter's volume decline illustrates the retention problem for mid-sized perpetual exchanges. Low fees and a listing on a large exchange can attract attention. Neither, on its own, gives traders a lasting reason to return.

Financial institutions move onchain and cross-chain swaps reopen

Chainlink and more than 50 banks launch Project Pangea to test T+0 stablecoin settlement for the $10 trillion FX market

Event: On June 23, Chainlink, Qivalis, a group of 37 European banks, and UniKA, representing more than 10 Korean commercial banks, announced Project Pangea. They aim to complete a live, legally compliant EUR/KRW stablecoin transaction within 12 months using same-day atomic FX settlement. Participating institutions manage more than $10 trillion.

Mechanism: Chainlink connects ISO 20022 messaging with onchain protocols, translating SWIFT instructions into atomic payment-versus-payment transactions on the dedicated Pangea layer-1 network. Direct settlement could reduce the spreads charged by intermediaries over a conventional T+2 settlement period.

Second-order effects: If bank-led stablecoin FX networks reach scale, deeper onchain liquidity in currencies such as EUR, KRW and JPY could lower quoting and hedging costs for market makers in FX perpetuals.

THORChain resumes cross-chain trading after five weeks and covers losses with protocol liquidity after the GG20 fix

Event: THORChain resumed cross-chain swaps on June 23 after a 39-day suspension following the May 15 incident at its Asgard vault, which caused $10.7 million in losses. Second-order effects: A single security issue halted this multi-asset network for 39 days, illustrating the operational dependencies in cross-chain systems. Proposed native XMR swaps also raise questions about how privacy features will fit regulatory requirements.
