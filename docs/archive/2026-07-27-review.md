### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: Hyperliquid’s advantage in weekly transactions still needs to be verified through position holding, fees, and independent traders. Aster’s vaults may trigger passive position reduction. Dango’s exit mechanism makes the final price source and withdrawal timing the primary risks.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: HIP-3 is attracting equity order flow, Aster is offering active strategies as a product, and CME’s regulated clearing and micro contracts are narrowing the gap with onchain venues.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: Multi-asset derivatives competition involves customer acquisition, capital management, and exit strategies. Dango explains that building its own chain can only amplify product paths, not replace sustainable revenue.
4. **Skeptics**: 54% of Hyperliquid assets are from a single week, and their long-term structure cannot be extrapolated. Aster does not have TVL or revenue performance data. CME also did not have complete transaction and position data on the first day of trading.
5. **Editor**: The four entities are responsible for handling order flows, platform exits, financial products, and traditional financial competition. There is no repetition of the main conclusion regarding RWA concentration reported on July 24th. The introduction does not overcrowd with details.
6. **Newcomers**: The text explains why HIP-3, strategy libraries, cash settlement, and phased shutdowns can change the risks borne by users.
