01

Equity perpetuals and tokenized stocks

November 12: Federal Register publishes Kalshi’s revised eligibility rules for perpetual futures on IPO stocks

Kalshi would allow eligible IPO stocks into its perpetual security futures market as early as the second trading day after listing, replacing a minimum one-month wait. At least 2.4 million shares must have traded since the IPO, and market capitalization at issuance must have been at least $3 billion. Existing requirements, including current market capitalization of at least $100 billion, still apply. The rules take effect November 19.

Perpetuals risk controls and execution

November 13th, KuCoin reduced the maximum leverage for six perpetual contracts.

KuCoin cut maximum leverage from 50x to 20x in the highest risk tier for the APR, BANK, AEVO, MASK, NEIROCTO and B2 isolated-margin perpetuals. It raised that tier’s maintenance margin rate from 1.5% to 2.5% and adjusted other tiers as well. The transition runs from 06:00 to 07:00 UTC. Traders with open positions need to check how far they are from liquidation.

November 11: Paradex adds an onboarding status to its request-for-quote API

In Paradex v1.171.3, the RFQ API returns NOT_ONBOARDED for accounts still going through onboarding. The interface shows a deposit prompt to new, unfunded accounts in place of a generic error. The release also fixes how the trade tape displays zero-priced options combinations, helping users distinguish account states from actual pricing issues.

Trading incentives

November 12th, Backpack launched 14-day perpetual trading rewards.

Backpack is allocating $2,500 per day from November 12 to 25, a $35,000 pool over 14 days. Crypto, equity and ETF perpetuals qualify, including trades placed by API. Users must register and hold at least $100 at each daily snapshot. The top 20 split half the pool, and other eligible users share the rest in proportion to trading volume.

AI evaluation and agent tools

November 12: Scale AI and IMDA release a Singapore legal reasoning benchmark

SG-LegalClarify tested 19 models on 1,860 Singapore legal scenarios, 760 of which deliberately omitted key facts. Web access raised the average score from 53.8% to 63.0%. When information was missing, about 39% of responses asked no follow-up questions. Even when models did ask, they covered only 39% of the relevant gaps on average.
