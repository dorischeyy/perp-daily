Hyperliquid real-world asset perpetuals exceed crypto volume for a full week, with single stocks contributing 61%

Event: Between July 13 and 19, Hyperliquid’s HIP-3 real-world asset perpetuals traded about $26 billion, or 54% of weekly platform volume. It was the first full week in which they exceeded crypto perpetuals. Single stocks accounted for 61% of real-world asset volume. This was day 45 of coverage since June 12.

Mechanism: HIP-3 allows third-party builders to deploy and make markets. The mix of trading suggests Hyperliquid is attracting equity order flow outside spot-market hours and traditional brokerage channels.

Second-order effects: Sustained growth requires more evidence than turnover. Open interest, fees and independent trader counts can show whether user demand is broadening. Concentration in single stocks also raises questions about licensing, corporate actions and pricing while the underlying market is closed.

ScaleTotal perpetual DEX volume was about $79 billion that week, with around $50 billion on Hyperliquid and $26 billion in its real-world asset contracts. The latter exceeded combined crypto perpetual volume at the other decentralized venues.

Dango winds down its perpetual DEX and Layer 1 in two stages less than four months after launch

Event: Dango announced that its exchange would stop perpetuals trading on July 29 at 12:00 UTC and its layer-1 network would shut down on August 13 at 12:00 UTC, less than four months after trading opened in April.

Mechanism: Positions still open at the deadline will settle at the oracle price. Vaults will unlock, with balances returned in USDC to spot accounts. Deposits left on the chain at shutdown will be returned to their original Ethereum addresses. Separate trading and network deadlines leave time for closing positions and withdrawing across chains.

Second-order effects: A dedicated chain allows an exchange to coordinate matching, settlement and incentives, but it also adds the fixed cost of running a network. The platform still needs liquidity, revenue and a viable compliance route. Procedures for an orderly exit should be defined at launch.

ScopeThe wind-down covers both the DEX and the L1. It includes mandatory position closures, vault unlocks, and the return of balances after the chain stops. No customer loss had been confirmed. Execution can be checked only after both deadlines.

Aster launches Vault Beta to connect managed strategies with perpetual market liquidity

Event: Aster launched Vault Beta on July 24. Approved managers can create actively managed vaults denominated in USDT or USD1, with users sharing profits and losses in proportion to their holdings. Managers can choose public, private or flexible position visibility.

Mechanism: Managers disclose lockups and profit sharing in advance. Withdrawals may force positions to be reduced or closed. The vault turns an actively managed trading account into a strategy that people can subscribe to without placing trades themselves.

Second-order effects: Strategy vaults can retain capital and generate fees while introducing manager selection, position transparency and withdrawal risks. A venue distributing managed strategies also needs measures of drawdown, capacity and withdrawal execution alongside TVL.

ImplementationThe product is in limited beta and only approved managers have access. Aster has not disclosed vault TVL, strategy returns, or actual subscriptions.

CME opens single-stock futures, extending regulated trading to nearly around-the-clock hours

Event: CME single-stock futures are trading. Its initial offering covers more than 50 stocks through 55 standard and 22 micro contracts, including Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Nvidia and SpaceX. This is day 18 of coverage since July 9.

Mechanism: Standard contracts represent 100 shares and micro contracts 10. Both settle in US dollars at the official close and expire quarterly. Trading is nearly continuous from Sunday through Friday. The contracts are jointly regulated by the SEC and CFTC, have a minimum 15% margin requirement and follow halts in the underlying stock.

Second-order effects: Traders now have another way to access extended-hours equity exposure or short stocks without borrowing shares. CME offers regulated clearing and micro sizing but retains expiry dates and weekend closures. Depth, corporate actions and pricing when cash markets close will increasingly distinguish it from equity perpetuals.

ImplementationThe contracts are trading. Their underlying stocks account for more than $200 billion in average daily notional volume and roughly 55–65% of the weighting in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100. Complete first-day volume and open interest for the contracts were unavailable.
