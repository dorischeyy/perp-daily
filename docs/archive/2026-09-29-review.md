# Editorial review for issue 91

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: In the perpetual futures market, there are signs of a decline in DEX share, cost advantages for Lighter, and differences in funding payments across locations. Coinbase and Citibank have integrated stablecoin payments into bank account systems. NVIDIA has moved agent isolation from application rules to runtime and chip-side controls.

**What is the most important finding?**: NVIDIA uses OpenShell to implement strategies, and then employs Sentry on independent DPUs for monitoring and isolating agents. This indicates that agent security is shifting from autonomous models to external execution boundaries where models cannot be directly modified.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The Robinhood Chain distribution line includes samples with Pons costs that are lower than average, and samples with lower than average emission levels. The frontier model evaluation line adds an independent control layer during runtime and on the chip side. The daily share of perpetual tokens on the chain has decreased from an average of 9.6% in recent weeks to 7.9%.

**What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating perpetual markets, it’s important to consider share amounts, execution costs, and funding payments separately. When evaluating launch pads, it’s better to focus on costs rather than just creating tokens. When deploying agents, it’s essential to isolate strategy execution from the model running environment, and to maintain the ability to shut down independently.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If subsequent complete data corrects DEX shares to recent averages, Lighter will lose its cost advantage in repeated large-order tests. The Pons cost metric proves to be non-comparable. Alternatively, NVIDIA’s platform may not be able to accurately identify violations during third-party tests. All of these judgments need to be downgraded.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Excluded routine perpetuals listings, price-only and ETF flow updates, payment partnerships with no account or settlement responsibilities specified, and general AI announcements lacking a mechanism or external verification.
