Drift rebrands as Velocity with a $127.5 million Tether credit line and a switch from USDC to USDT

Event: The Solana exchange Drift formally rebranded as Velocity DEX on July 1 in preparation for relaunch after its April 1 incident. The report attributed the $280 million theft to Lazarus; 31 transactions occurred in 12 minutes. Tether provided a $127.5 million credit line, with settlement shifting from USDC to USDT. The changes affected 128,000 users and more than 35 ecosystem teams. On July 3, team member Tracy Chow rejected criticism that the rebrand was an attempt to escape responsibility.

Mechanism: Affected users receive one transferable recovery token for each dollar of verified loss. The recovery pool started at about $3.8 million and can grow through quarterly exchange revenue, committed Tether funding and up to $20 million from strategic partners. Redemption requires the pool to reach $5 million. Holders may sell at a discount before then.

Second-order effects: Dreamcash wound down when USDT settlement became impractical on Hyperliquid, while Drift accepted USDT to secure recovery financing. The two cases show how counterparties can determine a protocol’s settlement currency. With DRIFT near historical lows, the time needed to reach the $5 million redemption threshold offered a public measure of recovery progress.

Grvt extends its perpetual DEX into a unified-margin investment platform

Event: A July 3 report from The Block Research described Grvt's perpetuals exchange as the foundation of an onchain wealth platform. Its unified margin design lets a single deposit earn interest, secure trades and provide access to institutional investment products. Monthly volume peaked in January at about $51.1 billion, or 15–25% of Hyperliquid's volume. Grvt reported about $111 million in TVL, more than 16,000 weekly active addresses and 67% weekly retention.

Mechanism: The platform combines four sources of yield: tiered promotional rates up to 11%, Aave integration, an 80/20 lending model and a GLP vault that attracted about $25 million in assets in the five months after its November launch. Keeping collateral productive reduces competition between trading and investment balances.

Second-order effects: With the largest exchanges extending their lead in trading volume, capital efficiency is becoming another basis for competition alongside market depth. Grvt's 67% weekly retention illustrates why monthly turnover alone cannot show whether users keep funds on a venue after trading.

Stablecoin supply contracts as tokenized securities expand

Stablecoin supply contracts for the first quarter in three years as funds move from Arbitrum to HyperEVM

Event: CryptoSlate reported on July 3 that stablecoin supply fell by more than $3 billion in Q2 to $312 billion, its first quarterly decline since Q3 2023. Yield-bearing stablecoins led the decline, with Ethena's sUSDe market capitalization down 52%. Bitcoin fell 14% in the quarter.

Mechanism: Demand shifted from crypto-native yield strategies into Treasury-backed products such as BlackRock's BUIDL and USDY. Stablecoin supply on Arbitrum fell 45% as funds moved to platforms including Hyperliquid's HyperEVM. Stablecoin transaction counts recorded their largest quarterly decline.

Second-order effects: Liquidity flowed toward a few ecosystems even as the overall market contracted. Inflows to HyperEVM suggest that a perpetuals exchange can attract liquidity to the wider network around it.

Securitize shares trade on the NYSE and onchain with voting and dividend rights

Event: Securitize completed its merger with Cantor Equity Partners II on July 2. SECZ began trading on the NYSE on Thursday, with fully tokenized shares also issued on Solana and Avalanche. The onchain shares remained tradable 24/7 when the NYSE closed for the US Independence Day holiday.

Mechanism: Under the issuer-sponsored structure, each token is the security itself, carries voting and dividend rights and can be removed from the US Depository Trust Company's custody system. Securitize said it had spoken to capital markets teams at several large banks and expected similar offerings within a year.

Overblocking by safety classifiers draws complaints about Claude Fable 5 after its return

Event: Anthropic restored access to Claude Fable 5 on July 1 after a US Commerce Department export restriction had kept it offline for nearly three weeks following a security finding by Amazon researchers. On July 3, Decrypt attributed reports of weaker performance to a new safety classifier redirecting legitimate coding requests to Opus 4.8.
