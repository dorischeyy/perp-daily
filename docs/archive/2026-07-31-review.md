# Editorial review

## A0 Reader value

1. **Why open this edition today?**: The CFTC and Hyperliquid are shifting the competitive threshold for prediction markets from expanding the number of participants to ensuring auditable settlement and access per contract. Mobee, on the other hand, is responsible for distributing local fiat currencies into blockchain-based stock tokens in Indonesia.
2. **What is the most important finding?**: The CFTC opposes broad template certification, meaning that outcome contracts cannot rely solely on parameterized market factories for scale. Instead, clearing data, dispute resolution paths, and regulatory materials must be developed as products.
3. **Which prior assessment changed?**: The statement has been changed from “Prediction markets can follow the same listing process as regular assets” to “Non-standard events must first become auditable settlement objects before becoming tradable markets.”
4. **What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating securities for trading, it is first necessary to determine the settlement sources, handling of exceptions, dispute resolution, and approval processes for each contract. Only after that can decisions be made regarding the ability to introduce new market participants.
5. **What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If the CFTC ultimately allows broad template certification without requiring detailed disclosure of clearing materials, or if blockchain-based platforms can expand their participation without any contract governance, then the priority for access infrastructure should be reduced.
6. **Which news recap was removed?**: Binance.US’ license plans that cannot be found, Coinbase’s quarterly reports for which no official materials have been released, CFTC committee schedules without meeting conclusions, and regular listing announcements and fee announcements have been deleted.

### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: Uniforming federal rules can reduce fragmentation in markets, but the final legality of transactions still depends on settlement sources, dispute resolution, depth, and actual risk management. Policy recommendations cannot replace this data.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: The scalability of prediction markets must be tied to contract-by-contract reviews, data source versions, and settlement evidence. Mobee’s advantage currently lies in local fiat entry points and on-chain deposits, but this is not proven liquidity.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: Both prediction markets and tokenized securities compete for trading users and collateral. The real competitive advantage lies in compliance and asset distribution infrastructure, not the number of news reports.
4. **Skeptics**: Hyperliquid Policy Center and Multicoin are stakeholders. Their joint opinions are not the final rule. Mobee has not disclosed any details about transactions or user numbers.
5. **Editor**: This issue does not contain any repetition regarding the price quality of equity perpetual futures from the previous two days, TEE executions, or conclusions regarding commodity options. These two topics are respectively related to regulatory approval and regional distribution.
6. **Newcomers**: The text explains why broad templates hinder regulatory scrutiny, and how tokenized stocks provide economic opportunities but do not equate to physical shareholder status.
