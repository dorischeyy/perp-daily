# Editorial review for issue 96

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: Sparkling and Copper added new routes into Hyperliquid. Sparkling targets individuals using self-custody, while Copper offers institutional permissions and auditing. U.S. community banks sued the OCC, bringing the question of crypto institutions’ access to federal bank credit before a court.

**What is the most important finding?**: Copper separates execution from control of assets. Hyperliquid executes trades. Copper handles MPC keys, multiparty authorization, collateral management and auditing. This illustrates the operational controls institutions need to access onchain perpetuals.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: Robinhood Chain’s distribution channels have shifted from Pons’ high fees to Arbitrum’s revenue. In August and September, the total commission was approximately 4.3 million USD. However, there is no information about how each application contributes to this revenue, nor is there any evidence to prove the quality of transactions and user retention after subsidies end.

**What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating new trading entrances, it’s important to verify distribution, key management, transaction execution, optimal execution proofs, and accountability separately. When assessing launch platforms, factors like cost increases, project graduation, long-term liquidity, and user migration should be considered separately.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If Sparkling launches its full web interface and reveals continuous transactions and retention, then the integration with Copper will result in verifiable institutional transactions. After the subsidies end, Robinhood Chain will continue to charge fees, and the Pons graduation rate will increase. Alternatively, the court may quickly dismiss the claims of community banks. Therefore, it is necessary to adjust the assessments regarding the entry layer, fee quality, and licensing issues.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Articles that duplicate information about ordinary stock tokens with True Markets, reports about PUMP that involve only digital supply but no method to calculate the actual value, perpetual tokens with no changes in the mechanism, and news with uncertain release dates or new developments have been deleted.

### Six reader perspectives

1. Top traders say that Sparkling, Copper, and True Markets have improved their interfaces or control mechanisms, but they have not disclosed real-depth information, slippage, open interest, or failure rates. Therefore, it’s not possible to infer the quality of execution based on announcements about integration.
2. Competing product lead: Retail self-custody, institutional multiparty computation and price protection address different needs. Competitive execution depends on the account experience, key permissions, routing quality and clear responsibility when something goes wrong.
3. CEO strategy: The fee growth of the Robinhood Chain can generate ecosystem revenue, but this revenue is highly dependent on transaction taxes and high-frequency issuance. It cannot be directly equated with sustainable user value.
4. Risk control and compliance: The OCC case has just been filed. Korean regulations are still being reviewed. Circle has only submitted a request for review. The text does not establish rules based on litigation, drafts, or opinions.
5. Skeptics point out that Sparkling’s partner size, Copper’s market share, ChainGPT’s testnet data, and Microsoft’s delays all stem from disclosures made by the entities involved. The context has been restricted in its disclosure.
6. Newbies: Each stage is clearly distinguished from the launched entry, public preview, beta, planned mainnet, feedback solicitation, and court filing. This prevents mixing different states into one completion process.
