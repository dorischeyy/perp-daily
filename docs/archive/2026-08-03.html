Weekly theme | U.S. classification of perpetuals as swaps or futures has tax implications

Event: CME CEO Terry Duffy warned that U.S. traders might lose the tax treatment common to futures if perpetuals are classified as swaps. The IRS has issued no guidance specific to perpetual contracts.

Mechanism: Regulated futures may qualify under Section 1256 for a 60% long-term and 40% short-term capital gains split. Swaps are more likely to generate ordinary gains or losses. The CFTC’s classifications do not bind the IRS, so otherwise identical trading rules could produce different after-tax outcomes.

Second-order effects: Competition in U.S. perpetuals could extend from licensing and clearing to tax certainty. Showing only trading and funding rates could understate the cost of holding a position. This assessment reflects CME’s warning and legal experts’ views. The IRS has not ruled.

Measurement limitsThe 60/40 tax treatment may apply to qualifying regulated futures. The IRS had not issued a classification specific to perpetuals. The reporting does not establish their tax treatment.
