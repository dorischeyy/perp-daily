# Perp DEX Daily editorial review · 2026-10-29

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: The trading platform simultaneously changes account rules, uses competition subsidies to acquire order streams, and integrates stablecoins into retail and institutional markets. Readers need to distinguish between distribution, matching, and margin capabilities.

**What is the most important finding?**: Coinbase is responsible for competition integration and rankings, but derivative orders that meet the criteria are actually executed by Deribit. Customer relationships and matching venues have been separated.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The judgment shifts from “competition based on asset expansion and fees” to “account models, reward formulas, execution backend, and governance jointly determining the real experience.”

**What decision can readers make?**: Product evaluation should first verify who executes orders, how rewards are diluted, whether margin funds are actually available, and who can restore account status in case of anomalies.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If the transaction occurs naturally 30 days after the competition ends, and more than 80% of the activity period has passed, with the actual deviation after the points calculation being less than 10%, and with the privacy restoration process not requiring the involvement of the governance party, then the judgment regarding stratification and dependencies needs to be adjusted.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Announcements that only describe cooperation visions, lack current capabilities, or user rule changes have been deleted. Similarly, candidates from seven AI vendors that exceed the window period, are repetitive, or only have service status have been removed.

### Six reader perspectives

1. Traders: The fact that OKX’s positions are not liquidated does not mean that orders are unaffected. Coinbase’s competitions also incur normal fees, spreads, and funding rates.
2. Competitor product leader: RLUSD has entered the deposit and exchange process, but as a collateral for Global Exchange, it has not been delivered yet. It cannot be considered as a collateral capability.
3. CEO and strategy: Coinbase controls customer access, while Deribit executes orders. Distribution and backend processes can be separated. Natural retention after the event still needs to be verified separately.
4. Skeptics: The Pacifica formula is not publicly available. Oracle relies on data provided by partners. Paradex’s emergency recovery depends on committees. All details are restricted.
5. Editor-in-Chief: The three platforms are responsible for account rules, competition distribution, and stablecoin integration. The three studies explain incentives, mixed execution, and privacy governance, without attempting to fabricate facts to meet requirements.
6. Newcomers: Each piece of text should answer questions like who executes orders, how rewards are distributed, what users have access to, and how to exit in case of anomalies. It’s important to avoid simply listing protocol names and technical terms.

### Discussion evidence

Pacifica’s recent third-party evaluations include promotional and alliance disclosures, but these cannot be considered as independent user verifications. GRVT and Paradex have not found any independent posts that meet the criteria of verifiable identities, complete context, recent usage, and actual capital utilization. ### New findings and open questions

Pacifica for the first time combines fixed weekly total points with opaque dynamic formulas. GRVT for the first time integrates mixed execution architectures with transaction volume, OI, and recommendation weights for evaluation. Paradex for the first time combines privacy-preserving proofs with committee recovery responsibilities within the same product framework. Activities that are retained after investigation, fault recovery mechanisms, proof of delays, privacy committee thresholds, fairness in professional channels, and actual exit times are also included.
