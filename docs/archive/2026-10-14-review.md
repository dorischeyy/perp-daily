# Editorial review for issue 103

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: Perpetuals are no longer limited to exchanges. They have also entered the packaging layers of private equity companies and institutional credit swaps. Corporate agents are beginning to use HR and financial permissions directly.

**What is the most important finding?**: 21Shares’ structure connects securities accounts, FalconX notes, Binance, and Hyperliquid perpetuals, smart contracts, and funding payments into a risk chain. Investors do not receive any equity from Anthropic.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: TradFi perpetuals have moved from ETF nesting risks to listing packaging layers. Agent governance has evolved from being isolated in test environments to being integrated into production systems, with identity management, approval processes, and audit trails being essential.

**What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating new derivatives or agent products, it’s important to consider underlying rights, counterparties, reference prices, variable costs, as well as permissions and capabilities in the real system.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If the product documents show real shareholder rights and data indicates that the system no longer relies on perpetual net exposure, or if the current version of Workday has completed policy checks before taking action, then the judgment regarding packaging risks and buffer times needs to be adjusted.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Promotions that involve ordinary perpetual products, mere changes in coin prices, and no new mechanisms have been removed. Also, old project sales that cannot be verified are no longer available.

### Six reader perspectives

1. A top-tier trader said that the 21Shares entry can identify funding payments and oracle risks. The PerpFinder entry must clearly indicate that market numbers and OI come from different platforms, and they cannot be combined into the same causal chain.
2. Product manager for competitors: S&P swaps combine authorized values with transaction lifecycles in the same ledger. Workday places permissions and approvals at the system recording level. Both designs are more comprehensive than simply putting things on the chain or using prompts as a safeguard.
3. CEO and Strategy: The main focus of this issue is that intermediate packaging is expanding distribution, while underlying risks are hidden deeper. When making decisions, it’s important to establish a clear table of rights and responsibilities.
4. Skeptics: 1win’s 100 million registered players, Google’s internal efficiency, Anthropic’s merger volume, and ChainGPT’s TVL are all reported by the company itself. This does not mean that these metrics have been adopted externally or replicated independently.
5. Editor-in-Chief: The first section covers retail ETPs, institutional OTCs, and market supply. Crypto and AI are responsible for ensuring supply chain security and production rights. The same event is not broken down into multiple sections.
6. Newcomers: The text explains that ETPs do not equal equity, on-chain swaps do not equal public order books, and embedded wallets do not equal risk-free operations. The context supports this conclusion.
