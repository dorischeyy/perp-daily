### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: The 724 stocks are just a coverage of the market, not a reflection of depth. The text separates Dinari’s valuation of about 10 million USD from its market value of about 2.2 billion USD, to avoid creating an illusion of liquidity through numerical comparisons.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: Tradable tokens are selling dividends and company action rights. If perpetuals only sell expansion speed, they will lose long-term user retention. Product judgments should focus on new features rather than general attention.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: Regulatory authorities, underlying rights, and collateral paths are being tightened simultaneously. The only priority is to ensure the integrity of asset-level rules before expanding the list of assets.
4. **Skeptics**: The three sets of unlocking calendars for PROVE conflict with each other. Therefore, the text does not selectively cite the largest numbers. Instead, the conflicts themselves are disclosed as a launchpad for disclosure gaps.
5. **Editor**: The articles about FWA, BlackRock’s new products in the US, and Coldcard have all been removed. Only Kalshi, Dinari, and BitGo remain as the main players. The detailed mechanisms are not discussed further.
6. **Newcomers**: The text explains what changes occur in federal priority rights, 1:1 custody stocks, resource pricing, and bridge control aspects. It does not require readers to understand the contract structure in advance.
