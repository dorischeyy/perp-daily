Perpetual futures and round-the-clock market operations

November 1: MEXC moves ASTER/USD1 perpetual funding to four-hour settlement

At 02:15 UTC on November 1, MEXC moved ASTER/USD1 perpetual funding to four-hour settlement. The cap and floor per interval are +3% and −3%. MEXC retains discretion to change the interval and limits again. Traders holding positions need to recalculate their intraday funding costs.

MEXC plans to process cash dividends on PFE, WFC, CCL, and HWM equity perpetuals on November 6. Contracts switch to reduce-only at 00:30 UTC, a special funding payment settles at 01:00, and trading resumes at 01:01. Longs receive the payment and shorts pay it. Onchain collateral and product design

DeepBook Predict uses a shared USDC pool for prediction positions with a fixed expiry

A shared USDC pool is the counterparty to every trade on DeepBook Predict. Roughly 800 milliseconds after submission, the order either fills at a Pyth-signed price or is refunded. LP subscriptions and redemptions also enter an asynchronous queue for batch settlement. The design can reduce front-running, while exit speed depends on idle cash and batch processing.

Reisa combines equity token issuance, trading, lending and stablecoins in one interface

Reisa offers bonding-curve token launches, a DEX, farms and isolated lending on Robinhood Chain. Liquidity is locked permanently after a token graduates. Equity tokens collateralize RESD. Protocol revenue funds yield and buybacks, while the asset allowlist, liquidation parameters and redemption depth still need verification.

Real World Lending leaves lenders directly exposed to defaults on equity-token loans

On Real World Lending, borrowers lock approved equity tokens and specify the USDG principal, interest and term. Lenders fund individual loans directly. They receive principal and interest on timely repayment or the entire collateral on default. There is no pooled capital or common rate, which also limits the ease of matching orders and diversifying risk.

AI products and agent operations

October 31: Agno outlines a deployment pattern for agent APIs

Agno demonstrates serving an agent through an API. Web, backend and mobile clients can reuse a session through session_id and inspect its execution trace. The example starts locally with SQLite and Chroma. Agno recommends Postgres and PgVector for production. Implementers must add authorization and tenant isolation themselves.

October 30: endue opens MCP agent calls and asynchronous sessions

endue’s MCP service allows assistants to list, call agents, and read sessions. If a task takes more than about 50 seconds, the session ID is returned first for subsequent queries. External calls cannot modify models, steps, or memories. Connectors and outputs are disabled by default, and sending or deleting actions requires approval.
