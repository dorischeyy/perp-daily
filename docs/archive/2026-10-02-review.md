# Editorial review for issue 94

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: Robinhood has integrated the Arcus stock token pricing into its wallet. Coinbase has moved perpetual trading to Deribit. This competition between perpetual markets is being driven from three aspects: distribution channels, underlying execution, and regulatory oversight.

**What is the most important finding?**: Coinbase’s migration isn’t just a change of brand; it involves a reconfiguration of positions, APIs, funding payments, and settlement times. This indicates that large trading platforms prefer to make derivative services more transparent.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: Robinhood Chain’s distribution process has shifted from being displayed within the wallet to being handled by third-party RFQ routing systems. However, Arcus perpetual futures is still in beta phase. Distribution delivery has only completed a portion of the stock token exchange process.

**What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating cooperation for trading interfaces, it is necessary to verify customer relationships, collateral, execution, funding payments, and fault responsibilities separately. Integrating wallets does not mean that perpetual products are already available.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If the Arcus wallet routing does not result in any new transactions, no decline in depth or availability after the Coinbase migration, or if the EU and SEC final rules reject the current proposal, then the assumptions regarding the expansion of infrastructure services and compliance entry points need to be downgraded.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Excluded routine listings, a DYNAMO subscription item whose original source was inaccessible, a benchmark originally published on September 19 and launch announcements without funding limits or adoption data.

### Six reader perspectives

1. Experienced traders say that Coinbase’s migration changes the way funding payments and interfaces are processed. However, the announcement does not include data on post-migration spreads, depth, or margin calls. Therefore, it cannot be concluded that the execution quality has improved.
2. Responsible persons for competing products: Both Robinhood and Coinbase have separated their front-end customer relationships from their underlying execution processes. It’s worth observing the distribution, collateral, and execution responsibilities as three independent competitive layers.
3. CEO strategy: The main focus of this period is not to increase the number of contracts, but to divide interfaces and responsibilities between brokers, exchanges, and regulators for both perpetual and institutional crypto services.
4. Risk control and compliance: HPC and SEC are merely proposals or advisory opinions. Traiq lacks a KYC process, and RunUp’s leverage funds lack complete access, stop-loss, and settlement disclosures.
5. Skeptics: The key numbers for Arcus, StonkFun, LlamaIndex, and Decagon all come from the same source or single research. The text does not extrapolate to conclusions for the entire market.
6. Newbies: Each category is distinguished by whether it’s already online, undergoing migration, being in the pilot phase, being proposed, or being in beta. This prevents confusing different stages of implementation as the same completion status.
