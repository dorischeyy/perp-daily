### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: CASHCAT illustrates why low leverage and isolated margin cannot substitute for order-book depth. Liquidity in compute perpetuals is still too thin to support an executable strategy. The article describes a launch rather than a mature market.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: The computing power pricing is not yet compatible with the new conditions of existing oracles. CASHCAT is more suitable for stress testing existing isolation pools, DPLs, and deviation checks. No additional recommendations are needed for existing mechanisms.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: The main point isn’t about adding a new asset, but rather that crypto perpetual structures are becoming the primary pricing tools for new assets. There’s a first-mover advantage, but the error index can turn this advantage into a risk.
4. **Skeptics**: The actual transaction volume and the proportion of hedging users for AX are not publicly available. The text states that “initial liquidity is still mainly speculative” and uses “implementation level” to define it. The current price of CASHCAT ranges from $0.05 to $0.065 at different points in time. The text uses a range, without fabricating single-point precision.
5. **Editor**: There were no repeat incidents such as Ostium’s money laundering, Summer.fi’s closure, Citadel’s investments, or Trasia’s micro-financing. The lead only provides price judgments, without repeating the previous events. Both main lines already have text and internal incentives, so the continuous tracking block at the top is omitted.
6. **Newcomers**: The article explains why compute cannot be stored, how funding supports continuous trading and why isolated margin does not prevent brief price spikes in a thin order book. It assumes no prior knowledge of derivatives.

### Top three revisions

1. The initial draft described AX as “decentralized perpetual computing”. After verification, it was confirmed to be an offshore exchange registered in Bermuda. It has been changed to “perpetual computing futures” to avoid misclassifying it as a DEX.
2. In the initial draft, the current drawdown of CASHCAT was mixed with the 60% drawdown from July 13. This has been separated into the current drawdown from July 17 and the background mechanism from July 13, to avoid temporal confusion.
3. After reviewing the market mechanisms, duplicate suggestions such as “increasing multiple price sources, isolation pools, and dynamic position limits” have been removed. Instead, recommendations such as temporary expansion of computing power and stress testing of existing parameters are proposed.
