Study finds concentration in MEV searcher rewards under winner-takes-all auctions

An August 5 paper examined MEV searchers alongside builders and validators. It used a contribution-weighted Jain index and an HHI of expected rewards. In a first-price, winner-takes-all auction, searchers with different capabilities may accumulate rewards continually through rank advantages.

The authors propose filtering admission and capping rewards using Shapley values. They acknowledge that replicated identities or strategies and validator collusion remain concerns. Open submission alone does not guarantee competition. Auction rules can concentrate order flow over time.

Measurement limitsThe findings come from a theoretical model with Bayesian safety constraints. A mainnet test would still need to measure latency, the cost of Sybil participation, and the new auction's effect on revenue.

Review of blockchain agent networks finds that a shared ledger cannot replace individual agent security

An August 5 review identified five risks in open agent networks: identity and capability, authorization and delegation, information provenance, collective robustness, and accountability and settlement. It examined where onchain identity, verifiable permissions and tamper-resistant logs can provide trust.

The authors describe blockchains as a shared trust layer. Verifying who performed an action does not establish its correctness. Semantic validation, privacy, reliable reasoning and individual agent security remain separate requirements for payments and onchain auditing.

ScopeThe paper reviews and classifies literature published from 1980 to 2026. It does not measure incidents or benchmark performance on production agent networks.

ExeCRE reduces erroneous changes to correct code by nearly 88% through consistency checks on feedback

ExeCRE runs candidate code on many random inputs and uses output agreement and a Dawid–Skene model to estimate reliability before deciding whether to invoke self-correction. It needs neither additional reference answers nor a model judge.

Testing GPT-5.2 on LiveCodeBench, the paper reported a fall from 113.2 to 14.0 in the average number of misleading feedback instances for already-correct code. This can curb unnecessary revisions, although inadequate random input coverage can give consistent errors high confidence.

Measurement limitsThe roughly 88% reduction applies to the paper's chosen baseline, GPT-5.2, and LiveCodeBench. Other languages, private repositories, and toolchains have not been evaluated on that basis.

WorldCycle reports up to 44% less state drift in world models using reversible action cycles

WorldCycle joins an action sequence to its reverse and requires a model to return to its initial state. This supplies a verifiable long-horizon reward without labels for future video, using both spatial closure and temporal consistency in training.

The paper reported up to 44% less drift on return to the original state and nearly four times the base model’s accuracy on compound actions. Closed loops supply some long-horizon supervision, but reversible sequences do not cover collisions, destruction or other irreversible interactions. Open-environment validation remains necessary.

Measurement limitsThe 44% and nearly fourfold results come from the authors' new closed-loop tasks and CycleBench. The tasks mainly involve reversible actions and do not measure all forms of long-horizon planning in open environments.
