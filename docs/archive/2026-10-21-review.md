### Six reader perspectives

1. Top traders/Big whales: The 24-hour market snapshot is a rolling window, not calculated directly based on the natural day of yesterday’s UTC time. Nominal transaction volume is not net inflow; it cannot be determined solely based on transaction/OI. Lighter does not involve settlement, but that does not mean there is no loss or the ability to exit with lower slippage at any time. The text retains fixed payments and full asset ownership. The details specify that selling orders depend on the depth of the order book. It does not use local historical data to estimate capacity, nor does it provide trading profit recommendations.
2. Competing product lead: Kraken synchronizes order submission, but fills can vary by account. Binance’s points promotion is not a newly launched real-money prediction contract. Compare DeepBook with Binance’s isolated-margin leverage mode without generalizing to every platform or presenting isolated margin as unique. Compare predict.fun’s own non-interest-bearing markets and explain buying, claiming interest and collecting settlement proceeds without implying that no other venue pays interest.
3. CEO/Strategy: The options available in this period involve determining whether to simplify directional transactions based on user tasks, as well as verifying exit paths before reusing margin funds. None of these three items can prove that the entire industry is moving towards the same model. AI operation costs are a separate category, and their importance should not be used to strengthen Crypto conclusions.
4. Skeptics: The official documentation supports product rules, but does not support independent use or effectiveness. The claim that MiniMax reduces costs by 70% is just a manufacturer’s assessment. Lighter and predict.fun lack independent testing, while DeepBook only provides named opinions rather than actual transaction results. It’s not possible to infer that the security policies that are officially available are effective in production.
5. Editor-in-Chief: The number of items has increased from 6 to 9, including new market transaction snapshots, Kraken desktop functionality, and Binance points activities. No single event is repeated to make up the total. The introduction focuses on the activity level of transactions on that day. News highlights changes, while research articles retain titles without dates. The AI-based items are still arranged in descending order according to the numbers 91, 89, 85.
6. For new users, DeepBook can be explained by tracking their savings, borrowing coins, trading, and repaying loans. Predict.fun can be explained by tracking interest earned from purchases, receiving settlement funds. Abstract judgments and unnecessary terms should be removed. The complexity of the mechanism should not be exaggerated. The source from 08-05 should still be clearly marked as historical background.
