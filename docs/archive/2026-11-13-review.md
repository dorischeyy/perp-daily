# Editorial review

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: Kalshi has pushed the earliest listing time for certain IPO stocks to the second trading day after listing. On the same day, KuCoin tightened the leverage and maintenance margin for six perpetual stocks. There was a similar change in the expansion of targets and risk capacity.

**What is the most important finding?**: Kalshi’s rules do not apply to all new stocks. It still requires that since the IPO, the stock has traded at least 2.4 million shares, with a market value of at least $3 billion, and a current market value of at least $100 billion. Quick listing does not mean giving up liquidity or meeting size thresholds.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The original judgment was that stock perpetuals mainly followed the expansion of listed large-cap companies. Now, it seems that regulated markets are starting to follow the pace of IPOs directly. At the same time, high market value and trading volume thresholds are used to control risks.

**What decision can readers make?**: Products and risk management can prioritize verifying the eligibility of new listings, the timing of parameter activation, the impact of existing positions, and the recovery of orders after a company’s actions. It’s not necessary to use increased listing numbers to replace complete risk management capabilities.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If regulatory rules are revoked, new stocks do not actually go public, parameter adjustments do not affect position capacity, or company actions result in discrepancies between balances and prices, then today’s expansion and risk management decisions should be downgraded.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Items related to ordinary perpetual tokens, duplicate tick size adjustments, model rumors without original sources, and items that only list product features without date increments have been deleted.
