## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: Variational combines zero-form fees, single market makers, and risk of bad debts into one mechanism. Coinbase’s data shows that a unified entry point does not equal increased liquidity.

**What is the most important finding?**: Variational’s OLP is both a trading partner and the only counterparty. It uses its own funds for external hedging. The product is simpler, but the risk concentration is higher.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The assessment shifts from “reducing interaction costs through unified liquidity entry” to “it is also necessary to separately evaluate rejection orders, price differences, and market maker defaults.”

**What decision can readers make?**: When unifying liquidity or aggregating multiple products, it’s necessary to consider factors such as account experience, real depth, trading partners, and margin settlement order priorities.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If independent data shows continued deeper transactions on Omni, or if Coinbase’s new perpetual transactions increase significantly, the current assessment needs to be re-evaluated.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Data related to token launches without incremental mechanisms, duplicate regulatory statements, and projects lacking new data from this period have been removed.

### Six reader perspectives

1. Traders: Zero explicit fees don’t mean zero cost. The real cost lies in the OLP spread, and OLP can reject orders during the final confirmation stage.
2. Market makers: A single OLP reduces liquidity fragmentation, but quotes, risk assessment, hedging, and bad debts are concentrated on a single counterparty.
3. The product: Coinbase’s unified brand and accounts have been established, but almost all open interest comes from Deribit. Therefore, integrating these channels cannot be considered as a complete liquidity migration.
4. Risk control: Independent settlement pools isolate funds for individual transactions, but this does not eliminate the risk that users cannot pay their debts after the OLP becomes insolvent.
5. Compliance: The FCA text is a consultation document, not a binding rule. The details specify the target and time frame, but it doesn’t establish new licensing requirements for all DeFi protocols.
6. Editing: Both PAPER and USTX use primary materials, but they lack independent depth, liquidity, or audit data. Therefore, the text retains these aspects for verification.
