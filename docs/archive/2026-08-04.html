Bitcoin’s annualized futures basis falls to about 3%, below the two-year U.S. Treasury yield

Event: CoinDesk reported that the annualized bitcoin futures basis had fallen from historical levels above 20% to about 3%, below the roughly 3.8% yield on two-year U.S. Treasuries. At publication, the basis had remained below that yield for 157 days.

Mechanism: Cash-and-carry arbitrage requires capital for spot holdings and derivative margin, plus trading, custody, financing and roll costs. A nominal basis below a lower-risk dollar yield leaves less compensation for committing institutional capital.

Second-order effects: A venue can make these products easier to compare by showing carry after financing costs and accounting for the risk budget across spot, futures and perpetuals. The 3% basis and 3.8% Treasury yield were annualized observations at publication, not figures for every maturity or venue.

Weekly theme | Venue design shapes systemic risk in perpetual markets

Event: In a CoinDesk opinion article, Bullish Exchange President Chris Tyrer challenged the claim that perpetuals inherently create systemic risk. He identified leverage limits, margin, reference indexes, funding and default procedures as the relevant variables.

Mechanism: In stressed markets, the sequence in which losses are absorbed depends on initial and maintenance margin, insurance funds, auto-deleveraging and the liquidation waterfall. Venues offering the same perpetual contract can therefore allocate tail losses very differently.

Second-order effects: A comparison of venues should include stress tests, index disruptions and default waterfalls alongside contract expiry. Tyrer’s employer, Bullish, has applied for U.S. designated contract market and derivatives clearing organization licenses. The article presents an interested operator’s view.

Weekly theme | More assets with observable prices are becoming perpetual markets

Event: Katana CEO Nathan Scott wrote in CoinDesk that perpetuals had expanded from crypto to equities, commodities and prediction-related underlyings. In his view, launching a market increasingly depends on access to a reference price and sustained trading demand.

Mechanism: Stablecoin margin and onchain liquidation let venues offer price exposure without transferring each underlying asset. Funding then anchors the contract to its index. EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations begin to apply

From August 2, EU rules require certain interactive and generative AI systems to inform users when they are interacting with AI and to mark generated or manipulated content in machine-readable form. Deepfakes, public-interest text without human editorial review and certain emotion-recognition uses also require public disclosure.

AI trading assistants serving EU users may need in-product notices, output provenance and machine-readable marks. Some existing generative systems may have a limited transition period for marking until December. Applicability depends on the system’s role and use.

ScopeArticle 50 applies from August 2. Some generative systems already on the market may qualify for limited transitional arrangements specifically for machine-readable marking.
