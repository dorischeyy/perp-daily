# Editorial review for issue 81

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: Bybit has expanded equity perpetual futures to include rights. The SEC is also preparing for full-day trading and experimenting with tokenized stocks on the blockchain. The CFTC is further clarifying the responsibilities of software entry points and regulated execution venues.

**What is the most important finding?**: The SEC’s temporary exemption does not provide general support for tokenization. Instead, it allows qualified venues to use price books or AMMs to trade tokenized NMS stocks, while retaining the issuer’s right to exit the market.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: Traditional-asset derivatives now extend beyond new perpetuals listings and volume growth to options. Changes to spot securities trading rules are also defining a regulatory route that onchain venues can test.

**What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating all-day TradFi products, it is necessary to separately verify aspects such as target coverage, cross-time pricing, portfolio margin requirements, issuer permissions, execution responsibilities, and clearing preparations.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If Bybit’s initial options do not have sufficient depth in the long term, and the SEC’s exemption does not lead to real trading activity and order flows, or if the CFTC takes no action regarding the software, then the judgment regarding the product’s expansion and responsibility distribution needs to be downgraded.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Old cases with no new rulings have been deleted. Interpretations related to old cases, as well as data drafts from exchanges with excessive marketing metrics, have been removed.

### Six reader perspectives

1. Top traders/big whales: Bybit only announces the first two targets and product structure. It does not provide information on depth, time-range spreads, or pressure scenarios. Therefore, it is impossible to determine whether the platform can handle large positions based on its launch announcement.
2. Product managers of competing products: Both the CFTC software’s stance and the SEC’s exemption for tokenized securities provide access, but they come with specific conditions, activities, and timeframes. It is not a blanket permission without any restrictions.
3. CEO/Strategy: Simultaneous implementation of full-day trading, tokenized stocks, and institutional funds on the blockchain. Opportunities lie in unified account and settlement connections, while risks arise from the division of regulatory authority and rights related to underlying assets.
4. Skeptics: The control rights of genius.fun, Cronos’s repurchase and destruction, and Capx’s automatic migration mainly come from project materials. It’s necessary to separate the mechanisms that have been implemented from the vision statements.
5. Editor-in-Chief: Four columns, three entries each. The Perp DEX column did not use standard coin listings to fill in the gaps. The Crypto column did not repeat yesterday’s Arc mainnet entries.
6. For new products, aspects like roundtable preparation, limited-time exemptions, RFP solicitations, enforcement actions, beta testing, and official launch are all distinct. However, the process status is not fully documented.
