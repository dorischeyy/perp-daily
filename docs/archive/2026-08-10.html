HIP-3 approaches half of Hyperliquid volume as quarterly protocol revenue falls for four quarters

Event: CoinDesk reported on August 9 that builder markets contributed about half of Hyperliquid’s volume and real-world asset perpetual open interest reached $3.6 billion. Quarterly platform revenue declined from approximately $357 million in Q3 2025 to $202 million in Q2 2026.

Mechanism: Builders can retain up to half the trading fees. The cost share of protocol revenue rose from below 6% in Q2 2025 to 18% a year later. Growth in third-party market volume therefore does not translate proportionately into funds available for buybacks.

Second-order effects: Permissionless listings expanded the product range, but the economics depend on fee splits, builder concentration and revenue retained by the protocol. New listings and user activity need to be considered alongside that net capture.

BYDFi reports open interest nearly 58 times its daily volume

Event: On August 10, BYDFi showed 113,363 BTC of open interest against 1,957 BTC of 24-hour volume, a ratio of about 57.93. It ranked eighth in the sample by open interest but last among major venues by volume.

Mechanism: Holding periods, reporting methods or data synchronization could explain high open interest alongside low daily volume. Without asset-level depth and liquidation data, a high open-interest ranking provides little evidence of liquidity available to exit.

Second-order effects: Such an extreme ratio calls for a data-quality check before it enters a risk-capacity model. If it persists for days while quoted depth remains thin, rankings will systematically overstate how much exposure the venue can support.

Toobit reports about 44% of Binance’s daily volume with roughly 14% of its open interest

Event: Toobit reported 53,602 BTC in open interest and 143,142 BTC in daily volume. Binance reported 397,519 BTC and 323,019 BTC, respectively. The corresponding open-interest-to-volume ratios were about 0.37 and 1.23.

Mechanism: Higher turnover could reflect short holding periods, frequent repositioning, incentives or differences in how venues report volume. Trading activity alone does not establish narrower spreads or deeper executable liquidity.

Second-order effects: Open interest, volume, spreads and incentives distinguish a high-turnover trading venue from one that carries risk over longer periods. A volume ranking omits information about outstanding positions.
