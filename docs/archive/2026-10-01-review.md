# Editorial review for issue 93

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: Hyperliquid introduces a third-party managed BVIV production market and institutions with qualified custody support for credit. Base also brings conditional transactions and B20 compliance control to the main network.

**What is the most important finding?**: With Ondo Perps, a spot stock token can serve as collateral for a perpetual on the same underlying asset. That expands the RWA comparison to capital efficiency and basis trades within one account.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The trading backend has been serviced by third-party organizations, separating market data and licensing responsibilities. TradFi/RWA perpetual markets have shifted from expanding asset lists to linking with spot collateral. Market rules are expected to progress from litigation and investigation to White House review.

**What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating trading infrastructure, it’s important to verify the fault responsibility of combined deployments, spot margin discounts, and institutional credit clearing boundaries. When evaluating compliant assets, conditions should be considered along with administrator permissions in risk management.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: Reassess production readiness if BVIV records no actual trading, Ondo spot tokens see no use as collateral, institutional credit cannot be issued repeatedly, or the CFTC rule is withdrawn without a public text.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Ordinary perpetual listings, token prices, simple model runs, new coins without refunds or funding boundaries, as well as launch costs that repeat from yesterday, have been deleted.

### Six reader perspectives

1. Active trader: BVIV’s dual-oracle design and safety mode do not establish actual spreads, depth or fills during abnormal market conditions. Those still need verification.
2. Competitor product managers: Ondo combines spot collateral with perpetual contracts in the same account. The competitive points have shifted from the number of coins to the use of funds and hedging methods.
3. CEO strategy: Institutions like Credit, OUSD, and B20 are integrating blockchain products into corporate or controlled asset processes, but there is insufficient data available.
4. Risk control and compliance: The CFTC’s entry into OIRA review doesn’t mean the rules have taken effect. Similarly, the MemeToro refund prototype doesn’t guarantee the contract’s security.
5. Skeptics: The claims of 500,000 HYPE coins, 1 billion USD in promises, and a 5-fold recall increase come from the entity’s disclosures. However, these claims cannot be considered as actual adoption without further verification.
6. For new users, each phase is clearly distinguished: launched products, public previews, test networks, and prototypes. The four phases are not mixed together.
