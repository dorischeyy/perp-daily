# Editorial review

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: MEXC’s regulatory risks have shifted from pending regulatory orders to actual restrictions on Binance’s deposit and withdrawal processes. The contraction of the capital channel occurred before the legal effective date on December 10th.

**What is the most important finding?**: Front-end trading doesn’t mean the exit path isn’t affected. Binance has rejected withdrawals to MEXC-related entities, and deposits from these entities are temporarily held for review.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The original judgment has been changed from stating that only FinCEN commands and MEXC’s normal operations could be confirmed, to confirming the date of publication in the federal register, the effective date, and the restrictions imposed on the largest competitors.

**What decision can readers make?**: Traders and product teams should verify that the platform can be traded, that transactions can be processed on the blockchain, that transfers between exchanges are possible, and that settlements with U.S. financial institutions are feasible. They shouldn’t use one normal state to cover all paths.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If Binance resumes two-way transfers, and other major competitors don’t follow suit, and MEXC can provide equivalent alternatives with no changes in transfer time and price differences, then the assumption that capital channels will continue to shrink should be downgraded.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Deleted ordinary tokenization processes, outright removal of certain tokens, duplicate tokenized stocks, and AI patches without a mechanism for incremental updates. Only those methods that will change the funding path, collateral efficiency, transaction distribution, or Agent evaluation are retained.

### Six reader perspectives

1. Top traders: The MEXC entry distinguishes between rejected withdrawals from MEXC and pending deposits into MEXC. It cannot be simply described as a generalized regulatory risk. The mortgage rate entry specifies the effective date, which helps to calculate the available loan amount and the remaining margin for liquidation.
2. Product manager of competing products: DriveWealth’s incremental approach involves the same European entity handling securities and crypto licenses, allowing partners to integrate multiple assets at once. This isn’t just another institution announcing its involvement in the crypto market.
3. CEO: Today’s most important operational signal is that regulatory orders have begun to affect the behavior of major counterparties. The status of MEXC’s front-end does not indicate that the institutional capital network remains intact.
4. Skeptical reader: Binance's restrictions do not establish that every exchange has severed ties with MEXC. DriveWealth's authorization is distinct from a live product. No independent production data were available for GRVT.
5. Editor-in-chief: Binance addresses both exit paths and capital efficiency. DriveWealth addresses distribution channels. GRVT addresses perpetual execution responsibilities. There’s no overlap between these categories.
6. Novices: Interpret the margin ratio as the conversion ratio of assets into margin value. Interpret GRVT’s structure as a centralized service that facilitates transactions, but the actions that affect funds still require wallet signatures and blockchain verification.

## A4 Project discovery and evidence

### Search coverage

The rotation covered GMX, Drift, Pacifica and GRVT, checking matching, oracles, settlement, liquidation, insurance funds, points, referrals and trader comments. GMX, Drift and Pacifica had no developments that outranked this edition’s candidates. GRVT documentation traced responsibilities from offchain matching and wallet signatures through onchain validation and insurance-fund intervention. Searches beyond the watchlist included “BNB prediction yield collateral” and “Chinese perp experience.” All six areas were checked: trading and execution, funding and collateral, real-world and new assets, distribution and compliance, user growth, and AI tools and evaluation.

### Project classification

GRVT operates under a perp-protocol model, utilizing offline matching and risk services. It handles transactions, transfers, and settlement verification using its own chain, and then submits transactions using zero-knowledge proofs on Ethereum. Binance’s margin mechanism is a centralized exchange-based system, while MEXC’s restrictions address counterparty and exit path risks. DriveWealth provides embedded brokerage and multi-asset distribution infrastructure. The three AI-related aspects correspond to model evaluation benchmarks, long-term Agent post-training, and Agent sandbox runtime.

### Discussion evidence

The main evidence for GRVT comes from official architecture and liquidation process documents. It is possible to verify the matching positions, fund signatures, proof submission, and the scope of insurance fund takeovers. However, no independent verifiable records of GRVT’s transactions, withdrawals, slippage, or liquidation experiences over the past 30 days were found. Therefore, discussions are left blank, and a discussion_gap is specified. The platform’s own performance is not considered as an independent transaction result.

### New findings and open questions

In the last seven issues, GRVT was not the research topic. This time, the execution speed and funding control were separated. The centralized service was responsible for matching transactions, the registration wallet was responsible for funding authorization, the own chain was responsible for verification, and the insurance fund was responsible for taking over positions when needed. It is necessary to investigate the recovery time for matching service failures, prove delays, the historical profits and losses of the insurance fund, the success rate of independent liquidation, the real-time withdrawal time, and the market-making concentration.
