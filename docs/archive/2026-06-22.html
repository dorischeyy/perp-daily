Weekly theme | CME v. CFTC tests whether U.S. perpetual contracts are swaps or futures

Event: CME alleged that the CFTC approved Kalshi’s first U.S. perpetual futures without its own analysis and that the approval order did not use the word “swap.” TD Cowen saw an advantage for CME and expected it to seek a preliminary injunction to pause the listings. This was an analyst’s forecast, not a court order.

Mechanism: Classification as a future or a swap affects margin, clearing, eligible participants and listing approval. The case could influence the framework applied to other U.S. perpetual products, including onchain contracts.

Second-order effects: A ruling could shape the availability of regulated perpetuals in the United States and the cost and feasibility of bringing onchain exchanges to U.S. users. The reporting trail includes The Block on June 19 and CoinDesk on June 21.
