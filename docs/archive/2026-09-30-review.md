**Why open this edition today?**: Aster integrated OTC negotiations into perpetual accounts. The migration of Reya’s order book revealed that the old stop-loss and take-profit limits were no longer effective. The UK crypto licensing process has officially opened, and OpenAI has enabled permanent agents to work across applications continuously.

**What is the most important finding?**: Reya retains its positions but cancels all previous conditions, indicating that the key risks of the transition lie not only in interfaces and liquidity, but also in whether user protection mechanisms are continuous.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The TradFi/RWA perpetual line has added industrial metals as a new asset type. The machine-readable market lifecycle line has been extended from the migration plan to order protection continuity. The predicted market federal rules line has added requirements for verifying identities of execution venues on the chain and for referring suspicious transactions.

**What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating new perpetual functions, it’s necessary to consider the protection against external transactions, risks during non-trading hours, and protection measures during migrations. When evaluating cross-application agents, it’s important to limit application permissions, approval actions, and to ensure a manageable working environment.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If Aster OTC does not result in any actual transactions, if Variational cannot maintain reliable pricing during traditional market closures, if Reya user protection measures are automatically restored, or if Dots’ cross-application permissions cannot be revoked with fine-grained control, all corresponding judgments need to be corrected.

**Which news recap was removed?**: New coins that involve simple leverage adjustments, lack of mechanisms, and no changes in price comments are deleted. Similarly, announcements with only model scores without any execution boundaries are also removed.
