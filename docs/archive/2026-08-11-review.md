# Editorial review

## A0 Reader value

1. **Why open this edition today?**: Transactions in major venues suddenly increase, but OI does not increase proportionally. This can help readers distinguish between short-term turnover, actual position establishment, and data anomalies.
2. **What is the most important finding?**: Binance, Gate, and Hyperliquid combined saw a daily transaction growth of approximately 131%. Their combined OI only increased by about 1.8%.
3. **Which prior assessment changed?**: The system now automatically isolates abnormal data summaries when there are two consecutive days of abnormalities. Previously, extreme OI/transaction ratios were manually reviewed as a reminder.
4. **What decision can readers make?**: Capacity and settlement parameters are initially determined based on executable depth at the asset level. Abnormal data summaries are used only to identify issues and are not directly used for risk assessment.
5. **What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If, over several days, the transaction volume decreases, and OI shows stable growth, or if asset-level evidence indicates that abnormal situations can be resolved effectively, then the isolation level should be reduced.
6. **Which news recap was removed?**: Ordinary currency price fluctuations, financing announcements without product mechanism changes, and generalized Agent accident summaries without authority boundary analysis have been deleted.

### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: A doubling in transactions does not equal a doubling in quote depth. All three Perp DEXs retain boundaries regarding asset composition, price differences, and unknown incentives.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: Jupiter’s dual-income strategy makes capital efficiency a selling point. However, losses from detaching assets are transferred to the collateral provider. It’s important not to compare only nominal APY.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: RWA’s new competitive dimensions extend from asset listing to revenue stratification and rights boundaries. The product matrix needs to record both economic exposure and legal ownership.
4. **Skeptics**: Extreme BYDFi ratios may stem from data sources and should not be interpreted as fraud or actual liquidity depletion. The text recommends isolating and reviewing these ratios.
5. **Editor**: The three Perp DEX items cover market-wide flow, changes in turnover onchain relative to centralized exchanges, and a persistent data anomaly. They extend yesterday’s static ranking.
6. **Newcomers**: The text explains how OI/transaction ratio, initial loss files, and Agent tool authorizations affect risk-bearing entities. These should not be inferred based on the title alone.

### Top three revisions

1. The initial draft only mentioned three cases of sudden increases in transactions. After review, the total OI increased by only 1.8%. The conclusion was changed from market expansion to a distinction between traffic and position differentiation.
2. The initial draft described BYDFi anomalies as insufficient liquidity. After review, these anomalies were reclassified as data isolation issues. It was also clarified that platform-summarized data cannot prove the ability to exit at an asset level.
3. In the initial draft, NextSlide was described as having been acquired on August 08-08. After reviewing the founder’s explanation, it was changed to an announcement on the same day, with actual transactions occurring earlier.
