Coinbase lists pre-IPO perpetuals on OpenAI and Anthropic while CME challenges U.S. perpetual listings

Event: Coinbase Markets launched ANTHROPIC-PERP and OPENAI-PERP at 11:00 UTC on June 22 through its Bermuda subsidiary. The report said Anthropic confidentially filed an S-1 on June 1 targeting Nasdaq and expected to raise more than $6 billion, while OpenAI filed on June 8 targeting a Q4 listing. U.S. users were due to gain direct access through a domestic product line on July 21. CME’s June 18 complaint expressly named CFTC approvals for both Kalshi and Coinbase.

Mechanism: These contracts reference implied private-market valuations. They give traders leveraged price exposure without delivering shares. Hyperliquid’s HIP-3 SpaceX perpetual, SPCX, traded $1.4 billion on the June 12 IPO date, 30% of the venue’s daily volume. That example suggests a listing could concentrate trading in OpenAI or Anthropic perpetuals if either company went public in Q4.

Second-order effects: Further AI listings could expand the market for pre-IPO perpetuals. Coinbase’s regulated product line and Hyperliquid’s permissionless HIP-3 markets compete for this flow through different routes. A preliminary injunction could interrupt Coinbase or Kalshi listings. Access, accumulated liquidity and user habits could influence where trading resumes. The edition expected interest in the sector to persist in the second half of 2026.

Taiko reports a $1.7 million bridge loss involving compromised key material

Event: Taiko reported a bridge incident on June 22 that resulted in roughly $1.7 million in losses. The Ethereum layer-2 team detected the incident within hours, paused block production and told users to withdraw bridged funds. TAIKO fell more than 20% that day to a record low.

Bank of England's June 22 stablecoin framework proposes a £40 billion cap per coin, drops individual holding limits and targets 2027 implementation

Event: The Bank of England issued a policy statement on systemic stablecoins on June 22. The proposal would cap issuance at £40 billion per stablecoin, raise the proportion of reserves allowed in short-term debt from 60% to 70%, and drop an earlier limit on individual holdings. Consultation runs through September 22. The Bank aims for implementation in 2027, after which qualifying stablecoins could circulate in the UK.

Second-order effects: The revised proposal gives prospective issuers more room than the earlier draft. If implemented in 2027, sterling stablecoins could expand the choice of collateral for derivatives and eventually support GBP-denominated margin for perpetuals. The implementing rules would determine whether that is feasible.
