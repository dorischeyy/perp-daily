New York court declines to halt state enforcement against Kalshi pending further review

Event: Federal Judge Jed Rakoff denied the CFTC’s request for an emergency temporary restraining order on Kalshi’s behalf, leaving New York able to pursue enforcement under state gambling law for the time being. The CFTC could renew its request before the assigned judge by August 7. The case concerns event contracts rather than Kalshi’s perpetual futures.

Mechanism: Kalshi and the CFTC argued that the federal Commodity Exchange Act gives federal regulators exclusive authority over commodity contracts on designated contract markets. At this stage, the court found insufficient evidence of likely success or irreparable harm. A federal venue licence therefore did not itself remove state distribution constraints.

Second-order effects: Without a stable federal preemption rule, state-specific access, age verification and review of contract categories can add distribution costs. The ruling provides an initial view on jurisdiction. It does not resolve the merits.

ScopeThe order addressed emergency relief and allowed a renewed application. It did not finally decide federal preemption or the classification of perpetual contracts.
