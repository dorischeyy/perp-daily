Regulated perpetuals and new market listings

November 3: Kalshi opens its first single-stock perpetuals after CFTC clearance

On November 3, the CFTC confirmed that single-stock perpetuals could trade on exchanges registered with both the CFTC and SEC. Kalshi opened contracts on Amgen, Costco, McDonald’s and Qualcomm the same day. It cautioned that the underlying stocks do not trade continuously. The regulatory path is open, but access still depends on the underlying market’s hours.

November 2: OKX adds five equity perpetuals with a provision for switching to hourly funding

On November 2, OKX rolled out USDT-margined equity perpetuals on ADI, FLEX, MELI, PEP, and UMC across its website, app, and API. Trading runs around the clock. Funding ordinarily settles every eight hours and switches to hourly settlement when a rate limit is reached. Listings are accelerating, but regional access and depth outside cash-market hours still need to be checked market by market.

Hyperliquid assigns oracle responsibility differently in native and HIP-3 markets

For Hyperliquid’s native perpetuals, validators submit a weighted median price from multiple exchanges every three seconds. On HIP-3 markets, deployers define the oracle, update prices and set leverage and halt-settlement terms. Traders use the same order and margin interfaces, but the responsible parties differ. A deployer can halt and settle a market at the current mark price. Slashed stake does not compensate traders.

DEX liquidity and fee design

Reya uses taker fees to subsidize makers after switching from an AMM to an order book

Reya moved from an AMM to an order book. Standard taker fees fell to three basis points and can reach two basis points based on trailing 30-day volume. Makers currently pay zero, and Reya plans to fund maker rebates with taker fees. Independent tracking counted five markets and volume about 23% below the initial launch period. Adoption data have yet to demonstrate deeper liquidity.

November 3: Apple opens its Siri AI actions preview to all developers

Apple has released beta versions of Siri AI for testing in systems like iOS 27.3. Any app can expose content and actions through App Intents, and multiple steps can be combined using skills. These features are not yet available to users. App Store submission will not be available until spring, and mobile systems in the EU are not yet available. Developers can now test the boundaries of interactions and behaviors.

November 3: Vercel adds AI source-code security reviews to every code push

Vercel Security Review is available for public testing. Every time GitHub pushes code, the system scans the code and assigns an AI-based severity rating. Vercel Agents can propose fixes and open pull requests, but approval from humans is still required for merging changes. This system does not perform runtime or penetration testing, and it charges based on the frequency of pushes and code changes. Therefore, it is suitable for continuous monitoring, but it cannot replace formal security audits.
