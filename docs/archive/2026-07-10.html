CFTC halts CME crude futures self-certification and receives a joint petition from Hyperliquid and Phantom

Event: In the seventh update on the CME–CFTC classification dispute since June 19, coverage resumes. On July 9, CFTC Chair Michael Selig halted CME's self-certification of continuous WTI crude oil futures sized at ten barrels. CME filed July 8 for a July 10 listing. Selig called that timetable wholly inappropriate and cited insufficient review time. The same day, Hyperliquid and Phantom, the wallet that acquired HIP-3 deployer Ventuals, petitioned the agency to exempt software development alone from broker registration, allow regulated derivatives firms to use blockchain infrastructure and exclude noncustodial wallets from broker classification.

Mechanism: The CFTC was asserting case-by-case oversight of continuous trading products while CME pursued its separate litigation over whether perpetuals are swaps or futures. Jenner & Block withdrew as CME’s counsel over positional conflicts, with former CFTC enforcement director Aitan Goelman taking over.

Second-order effects: The review demonstrates that a traditional exchange’s application can also be challenged. Hyperliquid’s petition sought clarity for developers and wallets before a judicial ruling. Pantera put decentralized perpetual volume at 14% of centralized volume, up from less than 1% in early 2023. Hyperliquid held about 40% of the onchain market and earned $13.5 million in weekly fees, roughly $1.9 million a day and fourth among crypto protocols. HYPE also entered the Bitwise 10 Crypto Index ETF at 0.93% as DOT and AVAX were removed.

A 400-day EUR/USD position on Ostium provides a case study in holding FX perpetuals

Event: In the second development in the RWA perpetuals series tracked since July 9, CoinDesk reported on July 9 that a trader had held a $1.139 million EUR/USD long on Ostium for 400 days, dating to June 2025. EUR/USD peaked at 1.2082 during the trade and was above 1.14 at the time of reporting. Annualized carrying cost was about 2.3%.

Mechanism: Ostium charges a volatility-based rollover fee in place of conventional crypto perpetual funding. This makes the carrying cost of a long-term directional trade more predictable and reduces exposure to sharp swings in funding. The design illustrates a decision that matters when adapting perpetuals to FX and other real-world assets.

Second-order effects: The position is one example of a customer using an FX perpetual for long-term exposure. Its limited size and the lack of comparable observations leave open how broadly that pattern applies.
