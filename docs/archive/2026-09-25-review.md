# Editorial review for issue 87

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: Equity perpetual futures begin to handle real-world company actions. Tokenized assets are being incorporated into customer funds and portfolio collateralization frameworks. AI agents also advance towards real-time visual interaction and verifiable privacy execution.

**What is the most important finding?**: OKX does not simply use stock splits as a means of price conversion. Instead, it handles old positions closing, new positions opening, liquidation price recalculations, and various types of orders. This is a clear example of how market lifecycle design transforms from trading operations to company actions.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The machine-readable market lifecycle series expanded from delisting, reduce-only trading and migrations to corporate actions. Robinhood Chain distribution coverage expanded from Bybit to a regulated Indonesian channel.

**What decision can readers make?**: When integrating non-crypto assets, it’s necessary to verify the complete mapping of company actions such as share splits to holdings, resting orders, profit-taking, liquidation, and accounting records. When evaluating tokenized collateral, it’s also important to distinguish between customer investments, margin qualifications, and final settlements.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If OKX makes adjustments that cause changes in position values or orders to be missed, Pintu’s coin adoption does not result in any new adoptions. Ondo tokens do not enter lending or perpetual collateral systems. Additionally, if the CFTC retires related regulations later, the judgment regarding the continuation of lifecycle and asset reuse today needs to be adjusted downward.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Ordinary perpetual contracts have been deleted. There are only market quotes with changing token prices. There are no fixed release dates for aggregated terminal pages. Additionally, there are no more detailed information about tokenized funds compared to Ondo, which has fewer mechanisms.
