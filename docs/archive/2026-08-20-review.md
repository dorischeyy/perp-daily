### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: A doubling of transactions does not mean a decrease in risk. A contraction in OI indicates that positions are being transferred or exited. The text retains information about funding rates, depth, and settlement directions.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: Hyperliquidness is a sign of compliance in the US, but it is not a license. The real competition lies in how non-managed protocols, front-end processes, and customer protection responsibilities are divided.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: If only one decision can be made, it should be based on a single collateral boundary. The risk associated with institutional credit should not be introduced into the settlement system just because USDe returns are diversified.
4. **Skeptics**: Rillet’s ARR, Also’s delivery schedule, and OpenAI’s privacy measures all come from company statements and should not be described as scale adoption or independent audit results.
5. **Editor**: The three Perp responses address overall turnover, CEX differentiation, and US compliance. The three responses are not filled with the same set of numbers.
6. **Newcomers**: The text explains the boundaries between OI and transaction ratios, credit tool limits, and final fiat settlement. It does not require readers to misinterpret the financing amount as the actual usage amount.
