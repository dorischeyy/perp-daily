# Editorial review for issue 108

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: The CFTC raised standing in the US perpetuals classification suit. JPMorgan brought its bank deposit token to Ethereum. Microsoft added dynamic permissions and independent blocking controls to agent governance.

**What is the most important finding?**: The CFTC and CME still have not resolved the issue of whether perpetual products belong to futures or swaps. However, regulators are beginning to use competitive damage and litigation eligibility to block such challenges. The subsequent court proceedings will directly affect the compliance expansion of perpetual products in the United States.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The US perpetuals classification series moved from product registration to the CFTC's response in court and returned to active coverage. JPM Coin added an example of public-chain settlement to the trading infrastructure series. The model evaluation series gained measures for dynamic permissions and external exposure.

**What decision can readers make?**: For US perpetuals, distinguish registration, actual trading and judicial classification. Evaluate access restrictions, dynamic permission revocation and monitoring of external tool calls separately for bank settlement and agent deployments.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: Revisit the assessment if a court finds that CME has suffered a legally cognizable injury, JPM Coin sees no institutional adoption onchain, dynamic permissions fail to reduce incidents or data on external agent calls cannot be reproduced.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Candidates with unlisted assets, single-price forecasts, missing release dates, or sources that cannot be accessed have been removed. There is no use of financing or brand promotion to increase the number of candidates.

### Six reader perspectives

1. Senior traders: PerpFinder can compare costs at the same time point, but it cannot be used to determine the optimal execution location throughout the day. The 60 markets listed as TRUE also lack transaction data, open interest data, and pressure data.
2. Product managers of competing products: FEAST, Debitum, and NEAR demonstrate the existence of entry points or activities, but there are still gaps in terms of graduation rates, user retention, secondary depth, and creator revenue.
3. CEO and strategy: The CFTC’s litigation stance, the expansion of JPM Coin’s public chain, and the scope of DTCC’s initial launch all involve distribution and responsibility boundaries. Priority should be given to legal and production adoption, rather than overall publicity.
4. Skeptics: BitTorrent, Notion, and Microsoft all contain information reported by the companies themselves or program-related content. The text retains data on audits, independent benchmarks, and production accidents, but there are still gaps in this data.
5. The editor notes that the most significant changes lie in the CFTC, JPMorgan, and Microsoft. All 12 items involve boundary interpretations, and there is no intention to allow ordinary financing, asset listing, or price manipulation to take precedence.
6. For new users: Perp uses a two-tier structure for events and mechanisms. Other sections allow a maximum of two entries per item. All important numbers are accompanied by descriptions of their magnitudes, implementation details, or general descriptions.
