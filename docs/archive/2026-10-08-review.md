# Editorial review for issue 97

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: Coinbase advanced the merger with Deribit towards product integration. Robinhood ranked first in September among those involved in tokenized stock transactions on the blockchain. GPT-6 improved the interface for direct interaction with the system. These three developments point towards a reconfiguration of accounts, assets, and software interfaces.

**What is the most important finding?**: Coinbase is preparing to integrate US and global crypto derivatives into a single regulated liquidity pool. What really matters is not the number of products, but whether unified margin requirements, cross-jurisdictional access, and settlement responsibilities can be implemented together.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: Robinhood Chain has shifted from high-frequency issuance and costs to a visible scale of stock tokens distribution. In September, the share of transactions increased to 42%. However, spot transactions still cannot replace perpetual contracts, independent users, and evidence of retention after subsidies.

**What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating trading platforms, it’s important to verify whether unified accounts actually share collateral and liquidity. When evaluating launch pads, it’s necessary to separate costs, commissions, graduation rates, and liquidity after graduation. When evaluating AI products, it’s necessary to separate manufacturer benchmarks from actual workflows.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If Coinbase fails to implement a cross-market liquidity pool, Robinhood’s subsequent transactions will likely decrease significantly, or it will be proven that these transactions are mainly due to re-lending. The cumulative data for Pons will not be able to be recalculated, and the Intelligent UI may not improve completion rates in real tasks. Therefore, it is necessary to downgrade the assessment for this period.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Excluded routine perpetuals listings, an old ChainGPT FAQ without a verifiable publication date, a late-September Comfy event presented as an October launch, and a policy interview offering opinions without any court or rule development.

### Six reader perspectives

1. Top traders: Unified accounts, expanded asset listing, and low fees cannot replace real market data, slippage, order rejection, and settlement data. The execution boundaries in the Perp column are retained for each item.
2. Competitor product leaders: Coinbase integrates accounts and collateral layers, while Binance expands its asset coverage. PerpFinder indicates that the same rate narrative can be distorted under different order sizes.
3. CEO Strategy: Robinhood stock tokens begin to demonstrate their value in trading volume, but Pons’ high fees may still be due to transaction taxes and high turnover rates. These two factors cannot be combined into a single conclusion regarding growth.
4. Risk management compliance: OKX and ICE have only applied for permits. Coinbase’s global liquidity pool is still in development. The USDG ecosystem is already live, but the actual risks involved remain unknown.
5. Skeptics: Pons’ accumulated digital assets, OpenAI evaluations, and AWS costs are attributed to the entity or second-hand disclosures. The context indicates that these cannot be generalized.
6. New projects: The text distinguishes between projects that have been fully integrated, those that are about to be launched, those that are already in use, single-point snapshots, regulatory applications, and open-source projects that are still under development.
