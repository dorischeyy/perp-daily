Cboe explores converting continuous BTC and ETH futures into perpetual contracts

Event: Rob Hocking, Cboe’s global head of derivatives, said the exchange was exploring a conversion of its continuous BTC and ETH futures, introduced the previous December with terms of up to ten years. The proposal followed more than $8.5 billion in volume at Kalshi within weeks of the CFTC clearing its perpetual product.

Mechanism: A long-dated future approximates continuous exposure through a distant expiry. A perpetual instead uses funding payments to anchor the contract to spot. Conversion would put a regulated designated contract market into direct competition with perpetual venues using its existing infrastructure.

Second-order effects: The June 19 CME–CFTC coverage now includes interest from Cboe alongside Kalshi, Coinbase and Kraken. An established futures exchange was exploring entry even as CME challenged the CFTC’s treatment of perpetuals and argued that swap rules should apply.

CFTC seeks public comment on physical energy perpetuals and 24/7 futures

Event: On June 22 the CFTC sought public comment on two changes: round-the-clock trading in conventional energy futures and perpetual contracts on physical energy commodities such as crude oil. Comments are due 30 days after publication in the Federal Register.

Mechanism: Extending funding-rate pricing to physical commodities requires a reference price that accounts for storage costs, pipeline capacity and delivery obligations. The consultation tests how a perpetual contract could track a market with those constraints.

Second-order effects: In a separate development in the CME–CFTC dispute, tracked since June 19, the regulator explored perpetuals on crude oil while CME challenged its handling of approvals. The legal status of existing products remained unresolved as the CFTC considered additional asset classes.

CFTC Chair Selig questions the use of perpetuals for physically delivered commodities

Event: CFTC Chair Michael Selig told the American Cotton Shippers Association that 24/7 trading and perpetual contracts do not suit every traditional commodity. He cited markets such as corn and live hogs that have limited trading hours and physical delivery requirements. His remarks qualified the agency's concurrent inquiry into crude oil perpetuals.

Mechanism: Perpetuals rely on continuous reference prices and funding payments and generally have no physical delivery. Seasonality, storage and delivery can weaken the link between funding and spot prices for agricultural and energy products, helping explain Selig's caution.

Second-order effects: In the policy debate tracked since June 19, Selig was the CFTC's only sitting commissioner at the time. His remarks suggested the agency might consider approvals by asset class and take a more cautious approach to physical commodities than to digital assets.
