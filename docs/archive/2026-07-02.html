Robinhood Chain launches with Arcus from the dYdX team and wallet integration for Lighter perpetuals

Event: On July 1, Robinhood, with nearly 28 million users in 38 countries, launched its Arbitrum-based Layer 2 mainnet. Uniswap and Pleiades deployed dedicated automated market makers at launch. Other announced integrations included the dYdX team’s new exchange, Arcus, offering perpetuals and commission-free trading in 95 tokenized stocks, with stock tokens usable as margin and support for pre-IPO markets, alongside Lighter perpetual trading, integrated into Robinhood Wallet. Lighter committed 11 million LIT to the Robinhood community and offered twice the points available on its own site. Robinhood covered gas and waived Lighter perpetual fees for the first 90 days. The dYdX Foundation said the existing chain remained operational and Arcus was a separate brand. LIT rose more than 20% that day.

Mechanism: Robinhood partnered with the teams behind Arcus and Lighter’s order book instead of building a new derivatives engine. Fee waivers, gas subsidies and double points reduced the initial cost of switching. A licensed broker could distribute perpetuals through a native wallet feature.

Second-order effects: Broker distribution adds another dimension to competition over fees and leverage. The dYdX team built a new brand for this channel, while Lighter committed tokens in exchange for reach. The June 30 coverage of Phantom’s Ventuals hires and Dreamcash’s settlement difficulties illustrates why access to order flow can be as important as market design.

Drift rebrands as Velocity with a $127.5 million Tether credit line and a switch to USDT settlement

Event: On July 1, Solana perpetuals exchange Drift Protocol officially changed its name to Velocity in preparation for a private beta relaunch. Tether offered a credit facility of about $127.5 million, and the platform switched its core settlement asset from USDC to USDT, affecting roughly 128,000 users and more than 35 ecosystem teams. The April incident, attributed to North Korea's Lazarus Group, caused an estimated $280 million in losses. Affected users receive one recovery token per dollar of verified loss. Redemption opens when the pool reaches $5 million. Funding includes existing protocol assets starting at $3.8 million, Tether's commitment and up to $20 million from strategic partners. Early redeemers receive their proportional share of the pool at that time and waive their remaining claim.

Mechanism: The relaunch combines a new settlement currency, a credit commitment from Tether and a compensation plan based on pro rata redemption. External financing supports the restart, while the pace of compensation depends on how quickly the recovery pool is funded.

Second-order effects: The April incident prompted a comprehensive restructuring: a switch out of Circle's USDC, a new external source of credit and redeemable tokens that spread compensation payments over time. Dreamcash, meanwhile, was winding down amid a shift toward USDC. Drift/Velocity's deliberate choice of USDT illustrates how a protocol's backers can shape its settlement standard.

Meituan unveils LongCat-2.0 after two months on OpenRouter under the name Owl Alpha

Event: On June 30, Meituan introduced LongCat-2.0 and identified it as Owl Alpha, the anonymous model that had led several OpenRouter rankings over the preceding two months. Its mixture-of-experts design has 1.6 trillion total parameters, about 48 billion active parameters and a one-million-token context window. Meituan described it as the first trillion-parameter model trained end to end on Chinese-designed ASICs. It scored 59.5 on SWE-bench Pro, ahead of GPT-5.5 at 58.6 and behind Claude Opus 4.8. Per million tokens, prices were $0.75 for input and $2.95 for output, or $0.30 and $1.20 during the promotion. GPT-5.5 cost $5 and $30, and Claude Sonnet 5 cost $2 and $10. Reads from cache were free.

Second-order effects: The reveal illustrates how anonymous commercial releases can complicate comparisons across model rankings. For teams running frequent agent or coding tasks, low prices and free cache reads change the cost comparison with GPT-5.5 and Claude.
