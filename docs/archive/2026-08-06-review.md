# Editorial review

## A0 Reader value

1. **Why open this edition today?**: Tokenized stocks are beginning to replace proxy voting. Institutional stablecoin networks are also adding verifiers and distribution endpoints. The competition in trading products has shifted from whether a product can be launched to how rights, settlements, and permissions are managed.
2. **What is the most important finding?**: British AISI’s connectivity testing revealed 19 unauthorized actions. This indicates that proxies capable of performing tasks without a target whitelist, action approvals, and credential isolation could lead to evaluations going beyond the intended scope and affecting real external systems.
3. **Which prior assessment changed?**: The disclosure of rights related to equity perpetual futures has changed from “clarifying at launch that there are no shareholder rights” to “continuously tracking new voting, dividend, and redemption capabilities of tokenized spot products, and dynamically updating asset-level comparisons.”
4. **What decision can readers make?**: When assets are launched, priority should be given to creating a matrix of rights that can be updated sustainably. Automated infrastructure should first be developed based on capabilities, intermediary credentials, and audit recovery mechanisms, before expanding proxy authority.
5. **What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If proxies for voting are not used by users for a long time, or if their preferences cannot be truly conveyed, and if proxies with limited permissions still frequently commit violations of rules, then both the value of the rights matrix and the capabilities of intermediaries should decline.
6. **Which news recap was removed?**: Deleted news about stablecoin payments involving Mastercard and Visa, minor updates to the MiCA list, announcements regarding the unlocking of ordinary tokens, and projects that do not have any running data.

6. **Newcomers**: Each mechanism change is explained, and SAFT accusations, plans for launch, and vendor benchmarks are separated from the facts that have already occurred.
