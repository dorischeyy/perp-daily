# Editorial review

## A0 Reader value

1. **Why open this edition today?**: Hyperliquid’s RWA perpetual expansion is now included in the agreement’s revenue retention framework, allowing for a clearer understanding of why transaction growth does not necessarily translate into agreement revenue.
2. **What is the most important finding?**: HIP-3 has contributed about half of the total transactions. However, Hyperliquid’s quarterly revenue decreased from approximately 357 million USD to 202 million USD. The proportion of revenue related to costs increased to 18%.
3. **Which prior assessment changed?**: Instead of verifying permissionless expansion based on transactions and OI, it is better to consider builder share distribution, revenue costs, and net retention under agreements.
4. **What decision can readers make?**: For third-party markets, net revenue retention is first verified in a single market. Only then can decisions be made regarding asset openness and liquidity resources.
5. **What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If subsequent quarterly data shows a rise in net revenue, a decrease in cost ratios, and continued growth in builder markets, then concerns about the economic feasibility of expansion should be reduced.
6. **Which news recap was removed?**: Entries related to general asset-based exchanges, generalized AI trends, and accelerators that only contain registration information have been deleted.

### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: The extreme turnover differences between BYDFi and Toobit suggest that withdrawal capabilities cannot be represented solely by OI or transactions. However, there is a lack of asset-level price differences and depth.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: HIP-3’s advantage lies in expansion and distribution, but the cost is builder share distribution and concentration. It is not possible to create market mechanisms without considering revenue design.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: The key indicator for new markets should expand from total transactions to net revenue retention. First, economic feasibility should be verified through pilot projects.
4. **Skeptics**: The decline in Hyperliquid revenue may be influenced by market cycles. The text did not attribute the entire decline to HIP-3.
5. **Editor**: Perp DEX’s three aspects—protocol economy, low turnover stock, and high turnover transactions—each addressed different aspects. No comparison with yesterday’s data was made.
6. **Newcomers**: The text explained why the builder share, OI, and daily transaction ratio affect the platform’s quality, and data boundaries were provided.
