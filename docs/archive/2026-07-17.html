Protocol closures after security incidents and Base's derivatives position

Perp DEX

Summer.fi announces permanent closure as recovery prospects worsen for stolen Ostium funds

Event: On July 15, seven-year-old DeFi protocol Summer.fi announced it would close permanently. A July 6 incident had cost its Lazy Summer Protocol vaults roughly $6.04 million and trapped team capital needed for rebuilding. The app was to remain available until August 31, with governance of affected vaults moving to Lazy Protocol DAO. About $4 million in user funds remained unresolved. Separately, The Defiant reported on July 16, citing PeckShield, that 10,540 of 12,086 ETH traced after the $18 million–$24 million Ostium theft had moved through Tornado Cash. Recovery prospects had worsened. This was day two of coverage since July 15.

Mechanism: Summer.fi’s experience shows how trapped operational capital can prevent recovery even after prompt disclosure. DeFi losses had exceeded $635 million in April alone. In Ostium’s case, subsequent transfers reduced the prospects of recovering funds.

Second-order effects: Offchain prices and onchain settlement create operational dependencies for perpetual and real-world asset venues. The two cases highlight the importance of independent reserves, insurance coverage, segregation of operating capital and an incident recovery plan.

Base founder acknowledges an unsuccessful social strategy and shifts focus toward perpetuals and prediction markets

Event: On July 16, Base founder Jesse Pollak said his bet on creators and social content driving crypto adoption had failed and that the market had collapsed. Base-native prediction market Limitless accounted for just 0.5% of July notional volume, and Avantis, the chain's largest derivatives DEX, ranked 18th by 30-day notional trading volume.

Mechanism: Pollak said Base would shift its focus to financial applications in trading, payments and AI agents. He identified perpetuals and prediction markets as areas where the network needed to catch up.

Second-order effects: A leading layer-2 founder's public acknowledgment of the gap suggests that derivatives have become a competitive priority for chains. More entrants also reduce the time early perpetuals venues have to establish lasting advantages.

Robinhood Chain launchpad Vlad.fun goes offline over internal integrity concerns

On day 15 of coverage since July 2, Vlad.fun, a Robinhood Chain memecoin launchpad, abruptly suspended operations on July 16, citing a serious internal integrity issue involving a team member. It retained legal counsel to investigate and did not disclose the nature of the issue. Hours earlier it had warned users about an impersonating token. The platform did not establish a connection between the events.

Two large launchpads ran into trouble in one week. Noxa cut its fees to zero and shut down on July 15 after its business model faltered. Vlad.fun cited a possible personnel integrity issue. Arcus's 35 proposed RWA perpetuals markets were still behind a waiting list 15 days after the July 1 launch.

Frontier model governance proposals

AI

DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis proposes an international frontier AI oversight body modeled on FINRA

In a July 17 Substack post, DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis proposed an international oversight body independent of governments and companies. Modeled on US financial regulator FINRA, it would review qualifying frontier AI models, open and closed, for up to 30 days. Independent technical experts of Turing Award caliber would lead its board, and major AI labs would fund it.

Hassabis said unexpectedly strong cybersecurity capabilities in a frontier model prompted him to publish early, citing concern over AI-enabled cybercrime and longer-term biosecurity risks. The proposal would establish a standing industry-funded reviewer independent of government. Other labs had yet to say whether they would support it.
