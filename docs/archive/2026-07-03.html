DYDX falls 23% after the dYdX team establishes Arcus as a separate company

Event: On July 2, after the July 1 Robinhood Chain launch, the market learned that Arcus was a separate company with its own token. dYdX founder Antonio Juliano moved from CTO to the board, while dYdX v4 was repositioned for maintenance rather than active development. DYDX fell 23% in 24 hours, from about $0.179 to $0.138. Volume rose 650%.

Mechanism: Robinhood’s partnership attached to a new brand and token. Existing DYDX holders had no automatic claim on Arcus, leaving them with less direct exposure to the distribution deal and future development.

Second-order effects: A broker partnership can benefit a new entity without raising the value of the original protocol’s token. The ownership of the new brand, token and development resources is therefore a governance question to settle before negotiating a distribution agreement.

eToro leads a $12.5 million investment in Extended to bring onchain perpetuals to its Zengo wallet

Event: On July 2, eToro led a $12.5 million investment in Extended with Jump Crypto and Alber Blanc. Founded by former Revolut executives, the StarkEx-based perpetuals exchange had processed more than $245 billion in cumulative volume. eToro plans to integrate its perpetuals into Zengo, the self-custody wallet it bought earlier that year for about $70 million.

Mechanism: Like Robinhood's agreements with Arcus and Lighter, eToro's arrangement pairs an investment with a planned native wallet integration. Traders would access perpetuals from the brokerage wallet while retaining custody through Zengo's multiparty computation setup.

Second-order effects: Following Robinhood on July 1, eToro became the second licensed broker in two days to combine an investment with a DEX integration. This could give smaller exchanges such as Extended wider distribution, while the broker controls the customer interface and much of the branding.

Bitget Stock+ adds U.S. equity options to its real-world asset range

Event: On July 2, Bitget added US stock options to Stock+, alongside tokenized equities and private pre-IPO investments. Eligible users could initially trade single-leg calls and puts. Multileg strategies were planned for later.

Mechanism: The maximum loss for an option buyer is the premium paid, giving traders a way to manage exposure alongside Bitget's stock tokens and perpetuals. Exchanges are competing on the choice of risk-management tools available for an asset.

Second-order effects: Competition in US stock and RWA trading now spans perpetuals, options and pre-IPO access. A DEX offering only perpetuals has a narrower window to differentiate itself through listings such as stocks or precious metals.

Visa, Stripe, Coinbase and BlackRock join more than 140 firms in Open USD, challenging existing reserve-income arrangements

Event: On June 30, Open Standard, led by Bridge co-founder Zach Abrams, announced Open USD (OUSD). Its more than 140 founding partners included Stripe, Coinbase, Mastercard, Visa and BlackRock. Circle shares fell more than 17% that day to a four-month low, down 55% from mid-May.

Mechanism: OUSD offers participating businesses fee-free minting and redemption. It proposes distributing income from reserve assets such as short-term Treasuries to partners pro rata after management fees. This differs from models such as USDC and USDT, under which issuers retain much of the interest.

Second-order effects: If OUSD gains traction, established issuers could face pressure to share more of the spread they earn on short-term debt backing non-interest-bearing tokens. Protocols using stablecoins for settlement or collateral also have an interest in who receives the income on those reserves.

OpenAI proposes giving the U.S. government a 5% stake worth about $42.6 billion

Event: On July 2, Sam Altman proposed to President Trump, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent that OpenAI donate 5% of its equity to the US government. At the company's latest $852 billion valuation, that would be about $42.6 billion. He proposed a public wealth fund resembling Alaska's Permanent Fund and invited Anthropic, Google and Meta to contribute the same percentage. The proposal was preliminary, and the legal mechanism for transferring private company shares to the federal government was unclear.

Mechanism: A donation would leave the shares in a new sovereign wealth fund intended to pay public dividends. OpenAI would receive no sale proceeds. The company could gain political goodwill from the arrangement.

Second-order effects: If Anthropic, Google or Meta followed suit, the US government could become a lasting shareholder in major AI companies. Ownership would give the government and industry a different relationship from lobbying alone, raising questions about how companies value closer alignment with regulators.
