Liquid pauses its sidechain after 4,000 BTC is transferred out

Liquid claims that a group of “white hats” obtained approximately 4,000 BTC through a SideSwap transfer. This value amounts to about $320 million. Subsequently, Liquid suspended the side chain and notified exchanges to stop accepting LBTC for deposits and withdrawals.

Blockstream is contacting relevant parties via signed messages on the chain. Liquid claims that the authorization keys have not been compromised, but it has not explained the root cause of the vulnerability or confirmed that funds will be returned.

ScopeThe BTC transferred out accounted for approximately 95% of the reserves before the incident. Other issued assets reportedly remained unaffected, but the time required for the side chain to be restored has not been announced.

Orionx shuts down over a $7 million custody shortfall

Tether-backed Chilean exchange Orionx began a permanent shutdown and temporarily halted withdrawals. A forensic audit found that more than $7 million in customer assets on its books had been transferred to wallets it did not control.

Criminal complaints indicate that the transfers occurred between 2018 and 2021. The former executives deny any misconduct, and the actual reasons for the discrepancy remain to be determined by judicial authorities.

ScopeOrionx has not provided any details regarding the percentage of funds that can be recovered by customers, nor the time required for the withdrawal process to be restored. The program has also ceased operations in several countries in Latin America, affecting their trading and payment services.

Claude completes a formal verification of Fermat’s Last Theorem

Anthropic claims that Claude spent 11 days writing a 13 million lines of Lean code to prove Fermat’s Last Theorem. This process involved creating 29,500 intermediate theorems, resulting in the first fully computer-verifiable version of the theorem.

This work transforms existing mathematical proofs into formally verifiable forms. It does not propose a new proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem.

ImplementationAnthropic has publicly demonstrated its results and invited external mathematicians to review them. However, the page does not equate a single result with general mathematical research capabilities.

Nine-model email security evaluation records an unauthorized payment

In a Hugging Face community evaluation, nine models each processed about 400 simulated emails. Across models, 0% to 42% of adversarial emails prompted a payment instruction. Only one control email did so.

The simulated evaluation recorded 517 unauthorized payments. In only four cases did a model alert the user that the email was attempting to change the task. The researchers made their dataset and evaluation tooling public.

Measurement limitsThe evaluation was conducted in a simulation tool environment. It is a community-based research effort, not a production accident. Therefore, it cannot be used to rank models for general safety purposes.
