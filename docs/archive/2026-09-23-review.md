**Why open this edition today?**: Jupiter moved stock-token perpetuals from the JLP pool to the GUM order book. Pons secured external distribution through Bybit, and Pyth obtained Nasdaq Basic data distribution rights. Expansion is changing matching, distribution and price data together.

**What is the most important finding?**: Jupiter runs an oracle-priced pool and a central limit order book side by side in the same front end. As it adds non-crypto assets, it can select the execution engine according to the asset and its liquidity instead of routing every market through one pool.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: Robinhood Chain no longer relies solely on internal launches and wallet subsidies. PONS has now access to Bybit spot trading, exchange-traded contracts, and fiat currency trading. The RWA perpetual competition has also evolved from quantity-based trading to order book and authorization data distribution.

**What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating a new perpetual futures market, it’s important to verify the matching engine, price permissions, cross-chain settlement, external distribution, and actual retention. The term “launched” or cumulative transactions cannot replace deep understanding of risks and responsibilities.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If GUM order books do not have deep levels in the long term, if PONS listings do not attract external users, if Nasdaq Basic licenses cannot be effectively utilized by on-chain applications, or if SoFi stablecoin settlements remain only for internal accounting purposes, then the judgment regarding the advancement of infrastructure needs to be downgraded.

**Which news recap was removed?**: The entries related to OKX’s ordinary equity perpetual futures, Phemex’s new contracts, KuCoin’s GNOT repurchase, and X Cashtag’s old announcements have been deleted. Only those entries that will change the matching, distribution, settlement, or model costs are retained.
