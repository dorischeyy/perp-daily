Avantis takes a share of profits on its zero-fee perpetuals

Avantis charges no opening, closing or borrowing fees on its zero-fee perpetuals. It takes a share of profits only when a trader closes a winning position. Losing trades still incur price losses and face a dynamic spread. The product currently supports BTC, ETH and SOL positions at 75x to 250x leverage. Traders save on holding costs but face a higher minimum leverage.

Drift routes orders through JIT auctions, onchain limit orders and an AMM

Market orders and immediately executable limit orders first enter a just-in-time auction lasting about five seconds. The automated market maker fills remaining demand if market makers provide insufficient quotes. Keepers monitor and match onchain limit orders. Under current margin rules, at most $100 of unrealized profit from any one position can back a new position, limiting the risk that paper gains in thin markets create across positions.

November 23: OpenAI and Cisco open-source Shirudo for temporary Linux protections

On November 23, OpenAI and Cisco released Shirudo, an open-source runtime that uses eBPF to load temporary protections without interrupting Linux services. Protections require signature verification. Operators can use a Trusted Platform Module (TPM) to record when protections are loaded or removed. Shirudo blocked an attack in a controlled Java4Shell test. The early-stage project is inviting developer contributions and still requires a permanent patch for the underlying vulnerability.
