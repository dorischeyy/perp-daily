U.S. community banks challenge OCC crypto trust bank charters in court

The American Independent Community Bankers Association sued the OCC, arguing that crypto companies should be allowed to use national trust bank charters to engage in a large number of non-trusting activities beyond what Congress has authorized.

The controversy centers around whether licenses allow crypto institutions to obtain federal banking credit, without them being required to provide deposit insurance, community reinvestment, combined regulation, or traditional capital liquidity requirements. Existing trust charters still cannot accept deposits or issue loans.

ImplementationThe case has just been submitted to the District of Washington Court of Appeals. The OCC has not responded yet. Currently, there are no injunctions or substantive judgments that change the approved regulations.

South Korea sets dual-node ledger rules and retail limits for tokenized securities

The Korean Financial Commission has issued a draft regulation that allows stocks, bonds, funds, and certain investment contracts to be issued and traded in the form of tokens. This regulation is scheduled to take effect on February 4, 2027.

The ledger must be maintained by a South Korean securities depository institution and at least two account management entities. The minimum capital required for the issuer’s account management entity is 4 billion Korean won. Retail investors are allowed to have a maximum annual net purchase of 100 million Korean won per over-the-counter exchange.

ImplementationThe draft was open for feedback from October 2nd to November 11th. It still requires review by the committee, legal review, and cabinet procedures. The parameters may be adjusted before it takes effect.

Circle urges MiCA to replace bank deposit ratios with liquidity requirements

In the EU MiCA review, Circle requested that the fixed ratio of electronic currency tokens stored in commercial banks be reduced to at least 30%, and 60% of tokens should be stored in commercial banks. Instead, a more flexible minimum liquidity requirement should be adopted.

Circle also opposed a 35% limit on a single sovereign asset and a rule limiting exposure to any one bank to 1.5% of that bank’s total assets. It argued that the combined rules would force large issuers to spread deposits across dozens of banks, potentially increasing operational and counterparty risk.

Measurement limitsThis is the policy proposal for issuing US dollars and euro stablecoins under the largest MiCA authorization. It is not a law text that has been accepted by the European Union. The effects of lower deposit requirements have not yet been disclosed.
