# Editorial review for issue 104

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: US stock tokens are now competing with perpetuals for the same users, who have full shareholder rights. Brokerage settlement systems are becoming more stablecoin-based. Long-term operations of agents involving thousands of agents require auditable supervision structures.

**What is the most important finding?**: Perpetuals is applying to use a regulated venue for dividends, voting rights and round-the-clock settlement. Those proposed shareholder rights distinguish this offering from synthetic price exposure and change how equity perpetuals compare with tokenized spot securities.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: RWA trading competition shifts from assets, leverage, and trading hours to rights and compliance distribution. Agent evaluation has evolved from pre-launch testing to ongoing retesting, termination, manual upgrades, and environmental security controls.

**What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating new trading entry points, it’s important to verify rights, settlement, and distribution status. When evaluating agents, it’s necessary to conduct operational supervision, manual upgrades, and minimum permission verification.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If Perpetuals fails to meet the exemption conditions or if there is no real adoption, DriveWealth will have no settlement volume. Third Moment’s failure rate and cost data have significantly worsened. Today’s structural assessment needs to be downgraded.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Updates related to VYX’s general functions, ChainGPT’s evergreen mechanism pages, OpenAI’s European adoption documents, and conventional coin listings that cannot be changed have been deleted.

### Six reader perspectives

1. Senior traders: Bybit’s matching system and KuCoin’s points may affect distribution and retention, but neither platform discloses actual matching funds, incremental transactions, or settlement performance. Therefore, these cannot be used as evidence of demand verification.
2. The head of competing products said that Perpetuals competes with rights integrity and compliance, while DriveWealth expands distribution with funds and settlement backend support. The difference in mechanisms is more worth considering than the number of new markets.
3. CEO and Strategy: The main focus of this issue is that trading competition continues to shift from front-end products to rights, brokerage settlements, and brokerage accounts. When making decisions, it’s important to clarify entry points, funds, and responsibilities.
4. Skeptics: The key numbers of Third Moment, Starlink, CryptoTotem, and TMM include self-reported or aggregated data. The text and context must be backed by clear evidence.
5. The main column contains three items: alternative products, strategy distribution, and retention mechanisms. No ordinary coin listings are used to fill in the gaps. The introduction only summarizes the three most significant changes.
6. New reader: The article distinguishes full shareholder rights from synthetic exposure, explains the risks that remain under self-custody and identifies what is live or still planned. A separate product assessment section is unnecessary.
