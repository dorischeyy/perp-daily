# Editorial review for issue 73

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: Aster’s one-time concentration backtracking removed approximately half of the current points. Robinhood Chain stock tokens began to accumulate liquidity. Both changes require dividing nominal growth into meaningful contributions.

**What is the most important finding?**: Aster isn’t simply a simple integration process. In reward settlement, it acknowledges that a few accounts can create distorted transaction contributions, which directly affects transaction incentives and the ranking system.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The statement “Transaction volume can directly represent activity contributions” has been changed to “Highly concentrated transactions must undergo an audit of account concentration and related behaviors before being eligible for reward settlement.”

**What decision can readers make?**: When designing trading rewards, it is important to first verify concentration, related transactions, backtesting windows, and complaint rules. Only then can decisions be made about whether to include transaction volumes in the Leaderboard or Merits.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If Aster’s public data proves that the removed transactions came from a large number of independent users, or if new rules lead to widespread harm or unsuccessful complaints from normal users, then the audit of concentration may not improve the quality of rewards.

**Which news recap was removed?**: The documents related to BitMart’s consultants, new coin launches, and general market reports have been deleted. These documents do not change the product mechanisms, distribution structure, or risk assessment.

- For top traders: The trade ranking of tradeXYZ is based only on a single-day snapshot. It lacks continuous depth, slippage, and processing during market closures. Therefore, it cannot be used directly to transfer positions.
- The product manager for competing products: Aster’s point-based system is worth evaluating, but the rules for account correlation and complaints remain undisclosed. It cannot be considered a mature anti-cheat system.
- CEO/Strategy: The stock tokens of Robinhood Chain have been added to the pool, but the number of tokens in the pool does not represent user conversion. Efforts should continue to focus onOI and actual transactions.
- Skeptics: LAPTOP’s instantaneous valuation comes from extremely thin liquidity, while Charter’s reduction in costs comes from self-reported data by the service provider. Both must be treated with caution.
- Editor-in-Chief: The introduction only includes three entities: Aster, Robinhood Chain, and Anthropic. Other mechanisms are left in the text to avoid making the summary into a table of contents.
- For new users: Anthropic’s events must be described in a testing environment and must not involve customer data. U.S. Bank must clarify that it remains a pilot program within the group.
