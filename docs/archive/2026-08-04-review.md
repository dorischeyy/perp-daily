# Editorial review

## A0 Reader value

1. **Why open this edition today?**: The annualized bitcoin futures basis was about 3%, below the roughly 3.8% yield on two-year US Treasuries. Perpetuals providers now need to demonstrate net returns and the ability to absorb risk alongside leverage.
2. **What is the most important finding?**: The risk associated with contracts with the same expiration date is mainly determined by the margining requirements of the venue, indices, and default cascades. The form of the contract itself is not sufficient to explain this fully.
3. **Which prior assessment changed?**: The display has changed from showing only historical funding rates and basis points to presenting fees, financing, and net carry after margining, alongside risk-free returns.
4. **What decision can readers make?**: Prioritize the development of net carry panels, abnormal index tracking, and liquidation waterfall disclosures. Then, expand to include more items that can be priced.
5. **What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If the basis of Bitcoin stabilizes above low-risk U.S. dollar returns, and the arbitrage capital flows back significantly after deductions, the urgency of net carry decreases. If there is no significant difference in losses between different venues under stress conditions, then the explanatory power of venue design needs to be downgraded.
6. **Which news recap was removed?**: Regular listing activities, rewards, and market news that do not involve any changes in mechanisms have been removed. All remaining entries have been supplemented with mechanisms, boundaries, or secondary effects related to the product.

1. **Top traders/major players**: A 3% basis point difference does not equal achievable returns. Costs related to two-way trading, financing, custody, margin, and rollover must be deducted. The text avoids referring to nominal basis points as net returns.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: Expanding the list itself will lead to commoditization. Competition will shift towards reference prices, net carry, abnormal handling, and distribution. Product evaluations will provide specific requirements for interfaces.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: BlackRock’s reserve assets, Bitget’s withdrawal from Japan, and EU’s AI transparency requirements all indicate that market opportunities and compliance boundaries must be designed together.
4. **Skeptics**: Both articles on Perp DEX were written by industry stakeholders and are not independent evaluations. The articles disclose the backgrounds of Bullish and Katana, as well as data gaps.
5. **Editor**: There are three entries per column. The leads are driven by actual figures for that day. There is no repetition of RWA transactions, U.S. taxes, Bybit collateral, or Claude evaluations from March 8th.
6. **Newcomers**: The issue explains the basis, carry, liquidation cascades, reduce-only trading, replace-by-fee and machine-readable flags.
