Cronos halts after Tectonic incident involving illiquid collateral

Cronos stopped producing blocks after an incident at its Tectonic lending protocol. Onchain researchers estimated roughly $75 million in affected assets and reported a roughly 100-fold price movement in thinly traded TONIC within about 20 minutes. The protocol had not confirmed a final loss.

The halt restricted cross-chain outflows but turned a protocol-level incident into a network-wide availability interruption. Tectonic had yet to confirm the amount lost or the cause, so the researchers’ estimate remains preliminary.

ScopeTectonic's TVL was about $121.7 million before the incident. A researcher said only about $6 million had been bridged to Ethereum. The protocol had not confirmed the amounts or subsequent disposition.

Polygon discloses node resource-exhaustion risk following a hard fork

Polygon said its Austin and Kyoto hard forks fixed issues in the Bor and Heimdall clients involving denial of service, validator resource exhaustion and checkpoint processing. It found no evidence of mainnet exploitation before public disclosure.

Patching before disclosure can shorten exposure. Nodes on older versions will fall out of consensus after activation, so operator readiness and timely upgrades matter alongside the code fixes themselves.

ImplementationNode operators were instructed to upgrade to Bor v2.10.0 and Heimdall v0.11.0. No known exploitation had been identified. The full effect of the vulnerability could not be quantified retrospectively.
