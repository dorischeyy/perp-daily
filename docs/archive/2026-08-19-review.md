# Editorial review

## A0 Reader value

1. **Why open this edition today?**: The trading volume and OI of the four major perpetual platforms decreased compared to the previous day. The United States is also promoting changes in stablecoin accounting categories and crypto issuance regulations. There are changes in capacity, collateral, and compliance that can be analyzed together.
2. **What is the most important finding?**: Hyperliquid’s trading volume increased slightly, but its OI decreased by 4.7%. Gate and Bybit both saw a decrease of about 12% in trading volume. This indicates that the overall volume declined moderately, but this was masked by differences in the quality of exits between different platforms.
3. **Which prior assessment changed?**: The criteria for access have changed from only requiring stablecoin reserves, redemptions, and on-chain liquidity to requiring institutions to have accounting qualifications for cash equivalents. However, existing risk thresholds remain unchanged.
4. **What decision can readers make?**: USDT deposits remain in place, as accounting proposals do not expand collateral. Later institutional distributions will first verify accounting qualifications, issuers, redemption conditions, freezing, and on-chain exit conditions.
5. **What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If the FASB ultimately withdraws the proposal, or if companies use data to show that classifying cash equivalents does not increase the willingness to hold and settle stablecoins, then accounting qualifications should not receive independent weightings.
6. **Which news recap was removed?**: Ordinary coin prices, ETF flows, company valuation rumors, AI features without a definite implementation time, and parts of the market’s post-closure predictions that have not been finalized have been removed.

### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: A 3.6% decrease in aggregated transactions does not mean that withdrawal is worry-free. The turnover rates of Gate and Bybit have decreased even faster. The text retains the gap in asset-level quotes.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: The improvement in the ratio of Hyperliquid comes from the shrinkage of OI, not from new leverage or share jumps. It cannot be described as a success in on-chain growth.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: Both FASB and SEC have only proposed guidelines, but they have changed the ways in which institutions can hold stablecoins and issue tokens for projects. This deserves consideration before entering the regulatory framework.
4. **Skeptics**: The procurement verification from Etched, the monitoring metrics from OpenAI, and the automated ratios from Warp all come from company-specific data and have not been written as audited scale measures.
5. **Editor**: The three entries for Perp DEX each address overall cooling, on-chain turnover, and CEX absorption. No duplicate entries were used for the same information.
6. **Newcomers**: The text explains the relationship between transactions and OI, the boundaries of cash equivalents, and the process layers of software factories. Readers are not required to fill in the mechanisms themselves.
