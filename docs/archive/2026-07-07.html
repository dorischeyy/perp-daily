Lighter sets Q3 target for token burns as LIT gains more than 50% in a week

Event: BanklessTimes reported on July 6 that Lighter had narrowed its June 30 guidance of several weeks after Q2 to a Q3 start. About 15.5 million LIT bought back since the token launch, equivalent to 6.3% of circulating supply, were to be permanently burned. Previously these tokens were held. Alongside the Robinhood Wallet integration, LIT gained more than 50% over the week and 13.5% in 24 hours, according to CoinDesk on July 6. Lighter recorded about $40 billion in trading volume over 30 days.

Mechanism: Lighter also shifted staking rewards from pre-launch subsidies to ecosystem reserves, targeting 6% annualized. On Base, Avantis had allocated 30% of daily opening, closing and profit fees to AVNT purchases and burned the tokens every six hours since March 11. Aster had operated for nearly a month with 99% of daily fees funding purchases and an equal burn. These are three distinct examples of fee-funded supply reductions.

Second-order effects: No Lighter burn had occurred by publication. Q3 remained the execution window, although the token had already appreciated. A delay or lower fee income would reduce the support behind the announced purchases. Both the burn date and future volume are observable tests of the market’s expectations.

Cointelegraph reported on July 7 that Strategy sold 3,588 BTC on Monday to raise $216 million for dividends. Futures recorded about $456 million in net selling in a four-hour window, and $91 million in long and short positions were liquidated as BTC fell from nearly $64,000 on Sunday to about $62,000. The report noted a concentration of leveraged longs in roughly $20.6 billion of market-wide open interest. That positioning can amplify responses to Federal Reserve signals. The volume of futures selling triggered by one spot sale illustrates the risk for other highly leveraged perpetuals markets.

June stablecoin volume reaches a record $1.79 trillion, with USDC at 67% and USDT at 32%

On July 6, Cointelegraph cited Visa's Allium-based dashboard showing $1.79 trillion in adjusted stablecoin volume in June, up 63% from May's roughly $1.1 trillion and 125% year over year. This edged past February's $1.78 trillion record. The measure filters high-frequency arbitrage and internal exchange transfers. USDC accounted for about $1.21 trillion (67%) and USDT $576 billion (32%). Base led with $565 billion, followed closely by Ethereum at $562 billion and TRON at $320 billion.

USDC widened its volume lead over USDT. Separately, USDT-denominated HIP-3 deployer Dreamcash wound down after Hyperliquid's native USDC integration made it difficult to match the user experience. The two developments illustrate how a venue's settlement currency can affect its competitiveness.
