Weekly theme | Hyperliquid policy group calls for common federal oversight and settlement standards for prediction markets

Event: On July 24, the CFTC asked designated contract markets to stop using broad templates to self-certify large batches of event contracts. On July 27, the Hyperliquid Policy Center and Multicoin backed a CFTC framework for prediction markets and called for a single federal regulator. This was the second update since July 24.

Mechanism: The CFTC asked venues to explain settlement methods, data sources and compliance with core principles for each contract. The policy submission proposed assessing restricted activities by what the contract actually settles on and publishing the reasons for admission or rejection.

Second-order effects: Auditable data, settlement rules and regulatory records affect the ability to offer event contracts. Hyperliquid already had onchain outcome contracts. The joint submission was a policy position, with no final rule or venue adoption established by it.

Measurement limitsThe joint submission was a response to a proposed framework. The CFTC’s July 24 document was procedural guidance on self-certification. Neither constituted a final rule, a licence approval or evidence of trading adoption.

Mobee brings tokenized access to 27 global stocks and ETFs to Indonesian users

On July 28, Indonesian exchange Mobee announced tokenized access to 27 global stocks and ETFs. Its product page says users can buy with rupiah or crypto and transfer tokens between the exchange and Web3 wallets on Solana.

The tokens provide economic exposure to the relevant shares or ETFs without granting direct shareholder status. The service connects local currency, exchange accounts and transferable onchain assets. Mobee had not disclosed trading volume, holdings or redemptions, so the announcement establishes availability and transfer routes only.

ImplementationThe catalog lists 27 stocks and ETFs, with deposits and withdrawals on Solana. The operator did not disclose user count, volume, positions, market depth, or the amount of underlying assets in custody.

Event contracts require auditable settlement before they can be listed at scale

Revised assessment: An event contract needs a settlement source, a dispute process and evidence of eligibility before a venue allows creation and trading. Ordinary asset-listing parameters cannot establish those conditions.

Evidence and limits: The CFTC opposed broad templates for certifying many event contracts at once. Hyperliquid-aligned groups proposed reviewing the actual settlement outcome and publishing reasons. These were policy positions, not a final adopted rule.
