### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: The increase in RWA transactions does not equal a deeper improvement for each market. What truly affects execution are the assets, trading periods, and platform concentration. BitMEX’s forced liquidation window directly affects the exit price.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: The AFX incident cannot be attributed to Arbitrum or the bridge contract code. It must be accurately described as a self-operated bridge signature failure. The RWA expansion cannot be simply compared based on market volume alone.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: $470 billion has been allocated to asset categories, but over 80% of this is concentrated on three platforms. This indicates that entering the market does not equal gaining order flow.
4. **Skeptics**: The 470 billion USD mentioned in The Block refers to transaction volume, not TVL, OI, or user count. The text and context have been carefully controlled to maintain accuracy.
5. **Editor**: The three paragraphs each address the issues of demand concentration, permission loss, and market withdrawal. There is no overlap with the Lighter collateral and Ostium restart rules mentioned on July 22.
6. **Newcomers**: The article explains why funds can be lost even when the contract functions as designed, why reduce-only mode precedes closure and why growing volume does not demonstrate a less concentrated market.

### Top three revisions

1. In the draft, the 470 billion USD was referred to as “on-chain RWA perpetuals.” This has been changed to a more comprehensive description that covers both CEXs and DEXs, along with an explanation of the concentration of the top three platforms.
2. In the draft, AFX was described as a bridge contract flaw. This has been changed to five hot signatures reaching the threshold. The contract logic and Arbitrum’s native bridges remain valid.
3. The initial draft treated BitMEX’s closure as a historical endpoint. The revision explains the sequence of reduce-only trading, forced position closure and early settlement.
