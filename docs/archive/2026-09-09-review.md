# Editorial review for issue 72

## A0 Reader value

**Why open this edition today?**: Hyperliquid’s total open interest has returned to 14.3 billion USD, but the proportion of HIP-3 has decreased from 34% to 25%. Pons has pushed Robinhood Chain’s daily fees to approximately 6 million USD. Both data points are changing our understanding of “where the growth comes from.”

**What is the most important finding?**: The increase in Hyperliquid total volume and the decline in RWA perpetual contracts occur simultaneously. This indicates that the total open interest of the platform cannot be used to replace the sustainability of asset categories.

**Which prior assessment changed?**: The original assumption that “RWA perpetuals expand proportionally with the platform’s total volume” has been updated to “RWA perpetuals are already large in scale, but new margin requirements will be reallocated to core crypto perpetuals in the short term.”

**What decision can readers make?**: When evaluating new markets, the total transactions and open interest should be broken down into three categories: asset types, market affiliations, and costs. This approach prevents confusion caused by increased quantities or overall platform metrics.

**What evidence would overturn the conclusion?**: If HIP-3’s open interest reaches over 34% of the total, and the nine new equity perpetual futures experience continuous trading and depth growth for a week, then the “return to core crypto perpetual contracts” will be just a short-term fluctuation.

**Which news recap was removed?**: Marketing activities on MEXC, new coin launches by Aster, and ordinary models without new mechanisms have been removed. This prevents the use of excessive numbers to fill in empty spaces in the report.
