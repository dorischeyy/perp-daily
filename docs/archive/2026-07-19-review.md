### Six reader perspectives

1. **Top traders/major players**: The prices of CXMT should not be considered as tradable assets. The lack of a cross-border arbitrage chain means that these prices may deviate from their normal levels after listing. Aevo’s maximum loss protection still relies on option transactions, which means there is still a risk of liquidation or liquidity issues.
2. **Responsible person for competing products.**: The Robinhood case requires separating the wallet interface from the local market. The difference with Aevo isn’t adding a new option, but rather turning the combination results into trading controls that don’t require understanding options terminology.
3. **CEO/Strategy**: The week’s finding is that venues can rapidly list perpetuals on new assets, but reliable price anchors and depth remain scarce. The first decision should be to test whether premarket contracts can transition safely when the underlying starts trading.
4. **Skeptics**: CXMT’s first-day volume was only a few million dollars. The text does not describe it as institutional pricing. Aevo’s content comes from corporate press releases, which has reduced its credibility score. It is also stated that no usage or execution quality data was used.
5. **Editor**: The first point focuses on the replication of pre-market perpetual futures from DEXs to CEXs. The second point focuses on distribution attribution. These two points do not overlap. Dune’s research only includes peripheral information and does not imply that centralized liquidity trading is the best approach.
6. **Newcomers**: The text explains why pre-market contracts lack stock rights, why external routes do not increase local depth, and how options combinations are hidden behind a result control.

### Top three revisions

1. The initial draft described CXMT as a regular news item, but the actual event started on July 15. It has been changed to a “main event of the week” that truly integrates Hyperliquid, MEXC, and Gate. The latest path on July 17 has been used as the main source of information.
2. The initial draft mixed the transactions of Lighter through Robinhood Wallet with Robinhood Chain’s native perpetuals. This has been separated into two layers: the distribution layer and the market layer, to avoid misattribution.
3. In the draft, Aevo’s “Limit My Loss” was described as a non-clearing protection mechanism. After verification, it was changed to a result boundary mechanism. Additionally, the depth of options and the risks associated with executing both options were mentioned.
