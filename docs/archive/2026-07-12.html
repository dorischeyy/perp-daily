Pump.fun’s largest unlock since launch is allocated primarily to its team and early investors

Event: Pump.fun unlocked about 8.25 billion PUMP on July 12, worth roughly $130 million at prevailing prices. About 5 billion were allocated to the team and 3.25 billion to early investors. The first major unlock since the ICO was nearly twice recent daily trading volume of $64–70 million.

Mechanism: Pump.fun was known for promoting fair launches without presales or extra team allocations. This unlock releases previously vested team and investor holdings without issuing new tokens. Pump.fun also allocates some daily fees to repurchases and burns. Earlier figures put cumulative burns at about 40% of circulating supply, offering a potential offset to selling pressure.

Second-order effects: The unlock tests whether fee-funded buybacks can absorb sales, a question also raised by Aster's program. A sharp decline within 48 hours would weaken confidence in buybacks during slower markets. A stable price would be consistent with ongoing purchases cushioning the release. The outcome requires post-unlock data.

The geopolitical risk premium fades

Crypto

Bitcoin falls less than 1% after Iran announces an indefinite closure of the Strait of Hormuz and the U.S. conducts its third round of strikes that week

Event: On July 11, Iran's Revolutionary Guard announced that the Strait of Hormuz would remain closed until further notice after striking a Cypriot-flagged container ship. The engine room suffered major damage and one crew member was missing. The US launched its third round of strikes that week, including at Bandar Abbas and Sirik. Brent rose for a third session to $78.80 a barrel. At publication, BTC was $63,784, down 0.45% in 24 hours. ETH fell 0.18% and total crypto market capitalization fell 0.32%.

Mechanism: A July 9 CoinDesk analysis noted that headlines about the Strait had previously moved BTC by as much as 5% in a day. Since the conflict began in February, moves had narrowed to around 1–2%. BTC had become more closely correlated with short-dated US Treasury yields than with oil, suggesting investors were assessing the conflict through its possible impact on rates.

Second-order effects: BTC holding above $60,000 as gold declined during a further escalation could be consistent with a shift between perceived safe havens. At present, low trading volume suggests limited panic without establishing that fresh buying supports the price.

Bitcoin and ether ETFs end an eight-week outflow streak, recovering about 3% of earlier losses

Event: The Block reported on July 11 that bitcoin ETFs recorded roughly $197.4 million in weekly net inflows after eight weeks of net withdrawals totaling about $8.26 billion. Ether ETFs also turned positive, drawing $84.4 million after approximately $1.2 billion left over eight weeks. Combined inflows were about $281.8 million.

Mechanism: The inflows recovered just 2.4% of the previous bitcoin fund withdrawals and 7% for ether. Monday accounted for $266 million of net inflows, Wednesday and Thursday saw substantial outflows, and most of Friday's inflows went to BlackRock's IBIT and ETHA. Overall trading activity remained low.

Second-order effects: The first positive week after eight weeks of withdrawals is encouraging, though the small recovery and concentration in a few large funds could reflect rebalancing. Another one to two weeks of data would help establish whether demand is broadening.
